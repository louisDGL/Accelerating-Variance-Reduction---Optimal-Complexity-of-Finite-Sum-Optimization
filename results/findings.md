# Findings (analysis of the numerical results)

Companion to `results/digest.md` (all numbers below come from `results/*_summary.json`, from which the digest is
regenerated by `experiments/make_digest.py`, except those flagged with the name of a cache, e.g. (B3.pkl), which were
computed from the cached runs `results/<TAG>.pkl`; the caches of B1, B3 and A2 are not versioned and are rebuilt
deterministically by their scripts). Conventions: R² = max_i (‖x_i‖²/4 + λ) is the per-sample smoothness constant, μ = λ
the global strong-convexity constant, κ = R²/μ; "rate per pass" means the contraction exponent −n·log ρ of the Lyapunov
function (or of ‖θ−η*‖²) per effective pass, ρ being a one-step contraction factor (the A3 note uses ρ for the certified
decrease instead, so Sections 5 and 8 write the certified one-step factor max{c_Z, c_Y, c_W} of L-Katyusha, defined in
Section 5); all one-step expectations are exact conditional expectations given the state. Trajectories are analysed only
while the Lyapunov value (‖θ−η*‖² in Section 2) is above 10⁻¹⁸ (SAGA) or 10⁻¹⁰ (L-Katyusha), well above the rounding
floors: the computed η* has ‖F′(η*)‖ ≤ 10⁻¹⁵ on every logistic problem except κ = 10, α = 1 (1.3·10⁻¹²), so that its
squared error is at most (‖F′(η*)‖/μ)² ≤ 7·10⁻²⁰ (to first order (‖F′(η*)‖/λ_min(F″(η*)))² ≤ 2·10⁻²¹), and the W-term of
Ψ, which multiplies F(w)−F* by 1500–2400 on the logistic problems, is resolved only to ≈10⁻¹³.

Gradient counts (Sections 6 and 7) follow the accounting of `vrlab/algorithms.py`: SAGA is charged n gradients initially
and 1 per iteration; L-SVRG, L-Katyusha and the naive scheme n initially, 2 per iteration (f′_i at the query point and
f′_i(w), the latter charged as recomputed although the table of the f′_i(w) is stored) and n per refresh of the reference
point (n·p = 1 per iteration in expectation), i.e. 3 per iteration in expectation. The bounds of the A1 note assume stored
reference gradients (2 per iteration in expectation), so the measured counts of these three methods are about 3/2 of their
stored-convention counts: ratios such as SAGA/L-Katyusha are affected by this factor, slopes in κ are not.

## 0. Validation (book setting, Figure 5.4 of Bach 2024)

With the book's normalisation (R² = max‖x_i‖², μ = R²/n, γ_SAGA = 1/(4R²), γ_GD = 1/R², SGD with γ_t = γ₀/(1+γ₀μt),
γ₀ = 1/R²), SAGA reaches F−F* = 10⁻⁸ after 13.6 (n = 1000) and 11.4 (n = 10 000) effective passes, and machine precision
(F−F* ≤ 1.1·10⁻¹⁶, one ulp of F* ≈ 0.5) after 28.3 and 24.5 passes (validation_book.pkl); after 50 passes SGD is at
4.6·10⁻⁴ and 3.5·10⁻⁴ (still decreasing slowly) and GD at 9.4·10⁻² and 0.14. The vertical axis of the book's Figure 5.4
stops at 10⁻⁸: the figure shows SAGA reaching 10⁻⁸, not machine precision, after ≈25 (n = 1000) and ≈11 (n = 10 000)
passes. Our n = 10 000 run agrees with the book (11.4 passes); our n = 1000 run is faster by a factor ≈1.8 (13.6 passes
instead of ≈25). The book's text only states that "SAGA gets to machine precision after 50 effective passes over the data
in these two cases", which our runs satisfy (within 30 passes). SGD and GD differ more: read off the book's figure, after
50 passes SGD is at ≈10⁻¹·⁷ for both n and GD at ≈10⁻²·² (n = 1000) and ≈10⁻¹·⁶ (n = 10 000), GD overtaking SGD after
≈13 passes for n = 1000; our SGD ends 1.7–1.8 orders of magnitude lower and our GD 0.8–1.1 orders higher, so that SGD stays
ahead of GD for both n. The qualitative picture for SAGA (linear convergence, many orders of magnitude ahead of SGD and GD)
is reproduced; the differences presumably come from unreported details (exact step sizes, data generation and seed). The
implementation is further validated by the unit tests (exact one-step identities checked by enumerating all indices, bound
verified in expectation along runs).

## 1. Hypothesis B3 — where the proof of Proposition 5.9 loses (Figures 1–3)

Setting: n = 1000, d = 50, κ ∈ {10, 10², 10³, 10⁴, 10⁵}, covariance spectrum j^−α, α ∈ {0, 1, 2}, textbook step
γ = 1/(4R²), Δ = 3γ², 5 seeds, exact instrumentation every n/10 iterations.

**(a) Size of the gap.** The ratio between the exact one-step contraction exponent and the certified one
(−log ρ_exact / −log ρ_bound, exponents averaged over passes 2–20, ratio averaged over the seeds) is 3.2–3.7 for κ ≤ 10²
(memory-term regime of the min, rate 1/(3n)), 5.1 (α = 0, 1) and 3.4 (α = 2) at κ = 10³, and then grows with κ in the
parameter-term regime: 29 / 16 / 6.0 at κ = 10⁴ and 220 / 100 / 19 at κ = 10⁵ for α = 0 / 1 / 2, i.e. like κ^0.87 /
κ^0.80 / κ^0.50 between these two values of κ. In this regime isotropic problems (α = 0) show the largest gap (for κ ≤ 10²
the three geometries are within 3.2–3.7). The late window (passes 20–100) gives the same picture (e.g. 187 / 72 / 11 at
κ = 10⁵). Figure 1 shows the same gap in the trajectories: at κ = 10⁴ (α = 1), E‖θ_t−η*‖² decreases by 11.4 orders of
magnitude in 100 passes (B3.pkl), while the certified bound decreases by the factor (1 − 3/(16·10⁴))^(10⁵) = e^(−1.875) ≈
0.15, less than one order of magnitude.

**(b) The exact chain reproduces the observed convergence.** The product of the exact conditional contractions along one
trajectory (violet in Figure 1) tracks the realized Lyapunov value (seed 0: within a factor 5.2 where the curves reach the 10⁻¹⁸ floor
(5.17 and 3.41) and within a factor 7.8 all along (ratio between 0.129 and 5.52 at κ = 10²), over a decrease of about 20
orders of magnitude at κ = 10² and 10³; within 6 % at κ = 10⁴ and 10⁵, and within 5 % after 100 passes; B3.pkl) and the mean over seeds: the
instrumentation is consistent and the whole gap is accounted for by the six inequalities.

**(c) Attribution.** "Share" of an inequality = ν_k / Σ_k ν_k with ν_k = log(ρ_{k−1}/ρ_k) the increment of the
contraction exponent (nats per iteration) when that inequality is undone, in the order B of the B3 note
(undo I5, then I4, I2, I3, I1 — the reverse of the proof order; the intermediate rates are the same as when
the inequalities are applied cumulatively in proof order, since the proof is a nested chain of substitutions).
Σ_k ν_k = (−log ρ_exact) − (−log ρ_bound) is the gap between the exact and the certified exponents; shares are
additive, not multiplicative (a share of 0.5 does not mean "a factor √speed-up").
* Memory-term regime (κ ≤ 10²): I1 (square norm) ≈ 0.39–0.44, I2 (cocoercivity) ≈ 0.32–0.37, I5 (min of the two
  rates) ≈ 0.07–0.22, I4 (strong convexity) ≈ 0.03–0.17, I3 (variance ≤ second moment) < 0.006.
* Parameter-term regime (κ ≥ 10⁴): I2 dominates (0.46 / 0.54–0.58 / 0.62–0.71 for α = 0 / 1 / 2), I4 is second
  (0.25–0.32 at κ = 10⁴ and 0.34–0.36 at κ = 10⁵, all α), I1 falls to 0.14 / 0.07 / 0.03 and I5 to 0.06 / 0.02 / 0.01
  at κ = 10⁵, I3 ≈ 0 (< 0.001).
* At κ = 10³ with strong anisotropy (α = 2) cocoercivity alone carries 0.83 of the gap (order A gives I1 0.58 and I2 0.22
  there: the attribution is path dependent).
* Over the whole grid, in order B, I3 never exceeds 0.6 % and the min step I5 never exceeds 22 % (seed averages; 0.75 %
  and 24 % for single seeds). Both bounds are specific to order B: in order A, which keeps the min until the end, I3
  reaches 6.3 % (κ = 10³, α = 2) and I5 carries 0.56–0.61 of the gap for κ ≤ 10², where I4 and I2 get a zero share
  (B3 note, Remark "reading the two attribution orders").

**(d) Tightness ratios (geometric means over passes 2–20).**
* r_var ≥ 0.958 (0.993–0.999 except 0.958 at κ = 10³, α = 2): the variance–second-moment step is tight because the mean
  stored gradient z̄ is small compared with the memory error (‖z̄‖² ≪ M/n).
* r_sq = 0.50–0.52 for κ ≤ 10² and at κ = 10³ with α = 0, where the memory is stale (the stored gradients date from
  iterates far from θ_{t−1}): the factor 2 of ‖a+b‖² ≤ 2‖a‖² + 2‖b‖² (a and b are nearly uncorrelated);
  r_sq decreases to 0.075 / 0.015 / 0.0035 at κ = 10⁵ (α = 0 / 1 / 2): once the memory is fresh relative to the
  slow motion of θ, ω_t ≈ F′(θ_{t−1}) and the proof's bound on E‖ω_t‖² by individual deviations is very loose.
  Its share of the gap is nevertheless small at large κ because the γ²E‖ω_t‖² term is second order for
  γ = 1/(4R²).
* r_coco = L_eff/R² ∈ [0.16, 0.37] (α = 0), [0.11, 0.16] (α = 1), [0.019, 0.18] (α = 2): over passes 2–20,
  R² = max_i L_i overestimates the cocoercivity constant actually experienced by 2.7–52×; the loss is largest for
  anisotropic features, where ‖x_i‖² varies a lot across samples and the maximum is far from typical.
* r_sc = μ/μ_eff decreases from 0.74–0.90 (κ = 10) to 0.012 / 0.027 / 0.14 at κ = 10⁵ (α = 0 / 1 / 2), i.e.
  μ_eff/μ = 81 / 37 / 6.9 over passes 2–20. This does not match λ_min(F″(η*))/μ = 70 / 26 / 2.1, the limit of μ_eff at η*
  along the slowest eigenvector: μ_eff/μ is above it by 16 %, 40 % and a factor 3.3, the iterates being still far from η*
  at κ = 10⁵ (E‖θ_t−η*‖² = 10⁻¹³ / 10⁻³ / 878 after 100 passes). With anisotropic covariance the smallest Hessian
  eigenvalue stays close to λ (λ_min(F″(η*))/μ ≤ 1.14 for κ ≤ 10⁴ when α = 2), so strong convexity is much tighter
  (r_sc = 0.79 / 0.46 / 0.14 at κ = 10³ / 10⁴ / 10⁵ for α = 2) and at κ = 10³ the gap moves almost entirely to
  cocoercivity (I2 0.83 against I4 0.11).
* r_min ≥ 0.9998 and r_var ≥ 0.958 although I5 and I3 carry up to 22 % and 0.6 % of the gap in order B (61 % and 6.3 % in
  order A): a ratio close to 1 can still cost a visible fraction of a rate that is itself close to 1; the attribution chain
  (differences of contraction exponents) is the right measure, the ratios are the right diagnostic of *why*.
* r_init (I6, initialization) = 3·10⁻⁵–0.03: the Lipschitz bound at initialization is loose by 1.5–4.5 orders
  of magnitude, but it only affects the constant (1 + n/4) of the bound, not the rate.

**(e) Interpretation.** The proof loses little in its "stochastic" steps (I3, and I1 at large κ in terms of
impact) and much in the two steps where global worst-case constants replace local ones: cocoercivity with
R² = max_i L_i (I2) and strong convexity with μ = λ (I4). Which of the two dominates depends on the geometry:
isotropic or mildly anisotropic features → both (I2 ≈ 0.46–0.58, I4 ≈ 0.31–0.36 for κ ≥ 10⁴, order B); strongly
anisotropic features → cocoercivity (0.62–0.83 for κ ≥ 10³). The min step matters only in the memory-term regime κ ≲ n.

## 2. Hypothesis B2 — trajectory-dependent constants (Figure B2)

* μ ≤ μ_eff(θ_t) on every record above the 10⁻¹⁸ floor (checked; smallest value 1.03μ, GD at α = 2, κ = 10²; below the
  floor, where ‖θ_t−η*‖² is at the precision of η*, GD reaches 0.79μ in that run). Along SAGA (one seed, θ₀ = 0), μ_eff/μ
  starts at 1.8–50 at θ₀ = 0, far from η*, where μ_eff is far from the Rayleigh quotient of F″(η*) in the direction of the error (at κ = 10⁴ the initial values
  50 and 21 for α = 0, 1 compare with the Rayleigh quotients 10.4 and 5.9 of F″(η*) in the initial direction), and ends
  close to λ_min(F″(η*))/μ at κ = 10⁴ = 10n: 9.94 vs 9.86 (α = 0), 4.97 vs 4.97 (α = 1), but 1.47 vs 1.14
  for α = 2, κ = 10⁴, where 60 passes do not reach the local regime (‖θ_t−η*‖² = 8.6 at the end) and μ_eff/μ is still
  decreasing. For α ∈ {0, 1} at κ = 10⁴ the initial error −η* is already concentrated on the single slowest eigenvector
  q_min (share π_min of ‖θ_t−η*‖²: 0.984 and 0.888 at θ₀ = 0); along SAGA the share first exceeds 0.99 after 9.2 and
  15.7 passes (0.993 and 0.980 at pass 10) and ends at 0.998 and 1.000. For α = 2 it stays below 0.003 (at most 0.0025, at κ = 10³) because many
  eigenvalues of F″(η*) are close to λ (29 and 11 within 10 % of λ_min(F″(η*)) at κ = 10³ and 10⁴: the slow subspace is

* Comparison with the predictions of the B2 note (Section "Direction dependence" and Remark "outcome of prediction (P2)"):
  prediction (P1) for GD (μ_eff non-increasing, → λ_min(H*)) is confirmed up to the speed of convergence (details in the

  see the next item) and refuted for κ = 10⁴ = 10n with α ∈ {0, 1}, where the error of SAGA concentrates on q_min as
  that of GD does: SAGA keeps and sharpens the alignment already present at θ₀ = 0 instead of spreading the error (per
  pass it aligns more slowly than GD for α = 0, π_min ≥ 0.99 after 9.2 passes against 1 GD iteration, and faster for α =
  1, 15.7 passes against 249 GD iterations); the three other runs, κ = 10³ with α = 1 (partial alignment) and α = 2 with

  most of the order of n, but κ ≈ n does not suffice. The note's quasi-stationary heuristic assumed that the noise
  covariance decays at the certified rate ρ_bound = 1 − 3μ/(16R²), slower than the slowest gradient-descent mode (1 −

  2–20 and 0.249 per pass over passes 20–100 (B3 summary: exact exponent and late speed-up), i.e. asymptotically at the
  rate −2n·log(1 − γλ_min(H*)) = 0.249 per pass of that slowest mode, the largest rate compatible with a nonzero
  component on it (B2 note); the noise therefore no longer outlasts the gradient-descent term and the error aligns — the
  theory-practice gap itself invalidates the pessimistic input of the heuristic, exactly the escape clause stated in the
  note ("a violation of (P2) […] would mean that the noise term […] decays faster than assumed"). This mechanism is
  inferred, not measured (the noise spectrum c_k(t) was not recorded).
* At κ = 10² the error does not align, and at κ = 10³ with α = 1 it aligns only partially: at κ = 10² (α = 1) the share
  stays below 0.05 (B2.pkl; 0.0008 at the end) and μ_eff/μ fluctuates between 1.38 and 5.45 (end value 2.15, geometric
  mean 1.90) while GD ends at 1.15 (λ_min(H*)/μ = 1.10); at κ = 10³ (α = 1) the share reaches only 0.31 after 49 passes,
  when ‖θ_t−η*‖² reaches the 10⁻¹⁸ floor (the five lowest eigenvectors then carry 0.88 of the error, and λ′/λ_min(H*) =

  makes this quantitative: SAGA ends at d_eff = 24 / 24 / 1.0 (α = 0, κ = 10², 10³, 10⁴) and 15 / 3.8 / 1.0 (α = 1), GD

  from one record to the next, and over the last five recorded passes it averages 18 and 17 for α = 0 at κ = 10², 10³
  and 14 for α = 1 at κ = 10²; B2.pkl); for α = 2 both stay spread (d_eff ≈ 12–15 at κ ≥ 10³) because the bottom of the

  recorded steps (100 % in 7 of the 9 configurations; exactly for a quadratic; the exceptions, 13 and 56 steps for α = 0
  at κ = 10³ and 10⁴, all occur at ‖θ_t−η*‖² ≤ 8.3·10⁻¹⁴ and 8.1·10⁻¹³ and are relative increases of at most
  7.2·10⁻⁷ and 2.7·10⁻⁶: rounding noise near η*, not an effect of the varying logistic Hessian; B2_summary.json, keys gd_mu_eff_increasing_steps,
  gd_mu_eff_increase_max_rel, gd_D_max_after_increase), and at the end of the GD runs (400 iterations or the floor) μ_eff/μ is within 2 % of λ_min(H*)/μ in 4

  4.7·10⁻⁹, 1.0, 5.7·10⁻⁴ and 2·10² (still far from η* in the last run), and at κ = 10² it reaches the floor before its

## 3. Hypothesis B1 — SAGA in a quadratic metric (Figure B1)

Setting: κ = 10³, α ∈ {1, 2}, P ∈ {I, diag(Σ̂)+λI, Σ̂+λI, F″(η*)}, step γ = 1/(4R_P²), 5 seeds.
* Empirical rate per pass of ‖θ_t−η*‖²_P (least-squares slope over passes 2–20, mean over seeds): α = 1: 1.00 (I),
  0.94 (diag), 1.11 (Σ̂+λI), 1.11 (F″); α = 2: 0.61 (I), 0.27 (diag), 1.26 (Σ̂+λI), 1.24 (F″). At α = 2 the statistical
  and Hessian preconditioners reach F−F* ≤ 10⁻¹⁵ (mean over seeds) after 26.6–26.7 passes, when plain SAGA is at
  ≈1.3·10⁻⁹ and the diagonal preconditioner at ≈1.8·10⁻⁶; at α = 1 the gain is small (10⁻¹⁵ after 28.8–28.9 passes,
  32.5 for SAGA) (B1.pkl). The diagonal preconditioner does not help — it is the slowest metric for both α — (features
  are randomly rotated, so diag(Σ̂) is nearly uniform while the scaling changes the step): Jacobi scaling is only useful
  for axis-aligned anisotropy.
* Transferred Proposition 5.9 with *global* constants (R_P² replaced, as in the step, by its upper bound
  max_i ‖x_i‖²_{P⁻¹}/4 + λλ_max(P⁻¹); μ_P = λλ_min(P⁻¹))
  predicts the opposite ordering: κ_P = 1000 (I), 1899 (diag), 5083 (Σ̂+λI), 3623 (F″) at α = 1, i.e. certified
  rates per pass 0.19, 0.10, 0.037, 0.052. The global constant μ_P is useless for a statistical preconditioner
  because λ_min(P⁻¹) = 1/(λ_max(Σ̂)+λ) is tiny.
* With the *local* constant μ_P = λ_min(F″(η*), P) (generalized eigenvalue) in place of μ_P — a heuristic substitution,
  justified only asymptotically as θ → η* (B1 note, Lemma "limits of the effective constants at η*"), so that the resulting rates
  are not certified — the rates given by the bound become 0.33 (I), 0.29 (diag), 0.333 (Σ̂+λI), 0.333 (F″) at α = 1: the
  preconditioned problems have local condition numbers 2.8 and 1, and the bound saturates at the memory term
  n·1/(3n) = 1/3 per pass. The observed 1.11–1.26 (Σ̂+λI and F″, α = 1 and 2) is 3.3–3.8× larger (3.34 and 3.32 at α = 1,
  3.77 and 3.73 at α = 2). Along the measured trajectories (exact one-step contraction of the B3 note transferred to
  the P-metric, instrumented states of passes 2–20, inequalities undone in the order of the proof; B1 note, Outcome)
  this factor splits into the memory coefficient, 1 − 0.65/n instead of 1 − 1/(3n) through r_sq ≈ 0.51–0.53 in the
  square-norm step I1: a factor 1.95–1.99 (52–55 % of the logarithm); the min step I5, in which the parameter term
  contracts 7–21 times faster than the memory: a factor 1.55–1.70 (36–40 %); and a pathwise remainder 1.10–1.12 (8–9
  %), which separates the measured slopes (seed-averaged least-squares slopes of log‖θ_t−η*‖²_P, i.e. rates of E log
  rather than of E) from the exact one-step contraction. None of this concerns E V_t^P: the bound controls E V_t^P ≥
  3γ²·E M_t, and the memory recursion of the B1 note (Step 2) gives E M_t ≥ (1 − 1/n)^t M_0 > 0 for the memory error
  M_t = Σ_i ‖z_i^(t) − f′_i(η*)‖²_{P⁻¹}, so that no bound on E V_t^P can certify more than n·log(n/(n−1)) ≈ 1.0 per
  pass, 3 times the certified 1/3, whereas along these trajectories the memory error itself decays at 1.12–1.25 per
  pass (B1.pkl), faster than its expectation can.
* Tightness ratios in the P-metric: r_coco rises from 0.13 (I) to 0.33–0.34 (Σ̂+λI, F″) at α = 1 — the statistical
  metric homogenises the per-sample curvature (leverage scores) — while r_sc (with the global μ_P) falls to
  0.017–0.023: in the P-metric the gap moves from cocoercivity to the global strong-convexity constant, exactly
  as the B1 note predicts. r_var ≥ 0.958 in all metrics; r_sq is 0.38–0.53 except for P = I and diag at α = 2
  (0.21 and 0.087). The other predictions of the B1 note on the ratios, (P4), are only partly confirmed: r_init increases
  under preconditioning, as predicted (8.5·10⁻⁴, 1.2·10⁻³, 3.7·10⁻³, 7.3·10⁻³ for I, diag, Σ̂+λI, F″ at α = 1; B1.pkl),
  but r_var increases instead of decreasing (0.9936, 0.9956, 0.9990, 0.9990 at α = 1; 0.958, 0.970, 0.9991, 0.9984 at
  α = 2) and r_sq exceeds ½ for the dense metrics (0.525 and 0.526 at α = 1, 0.507 and 0.509 at α = 2), i.e. the
  correlation of a_{t,i} and b_{t,i} is positive there instead of negative: these two predictions are refuted for Σ̂+λI
  and F″. Both failures occur in the memory-bottleneck regime, where the stored gradients were computed at past iterates
  much farther from η* than θ_{t−1}, contrary to the comparable past errors assumed by the heuristic: over passes 15–20,
  1 − μ_eff^P/L_eff^P is 0.96 (α = 1) and 0.81 (α = 2) for Σ̂+λI while r_var ≈ 0.9995–0.9998, whereas for P = I and diag
