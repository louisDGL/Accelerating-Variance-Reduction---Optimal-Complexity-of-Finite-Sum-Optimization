# Findings (analysis of the numerical results)

Companion to `results/digest.md` (all numbers below come from `results/*_summary.json`; the digest is regenerated by
`experiments/make_digest.py`). Conventions: R² = max_i (‖x_i‖²/4 + λ) is the per-sample smoothness constant,
μ = λ the global strong-convexity constant, κ = R²/μ; "rate per pass" means the contraction exponent
−n·log ρ of the Lyapunov function (or of ‖θ−η*‖²) per effective pass; all one-step expectations are exact
conditional expectations given the state. Trajectories are analysed only while the Lyapunov value is above
10⁻¹⁸ (SAGA) or 10⁻¹⁰ (L-Katyusha), well above the rounding floors (≈10⁻²³ for ‖θ−η*‖² given ‖F′(η*)‖ ≤ 10⁻¹⁵,
and ≈10⁻¹³ for the W-term of Ψ, which multiplies F(w)−F* by ≈1500).

## 0. Validation (book setting, Figure 5.4 of Bach 2024)

With the book's normalisation (R² = max‖x_i‖², μ = R²/n, γ_SAGA = 1/(4R²), γ_GD = 1/R²), SAGA reaches
F−F* = 10⁻⁸ after 13.6 (n = 1000) and 11.4 (n = 10 000) effective passes and machine precision (10⁻¹⁶) within
30 passes; SGD plateaus around 10⁻³·⁵ and GD is at 10⁻¹ after 50 passes. The book reports SAGA at machine
precision after ≈25 (n = 1000) and ≈10 (n = 10 000) passes with GD at ≈10⁻² — same qualitative picture; the
remaining differences come from unreported details (exact step sizes, data seed). The implementation is further
validated by the unit tests (exact one-step identities checked by enumerating all indices, bound verified in
expectation along runs).

## 1. Hypothesis B3 — where the proof of Proposition 5.9 loses (Figures 1–3)

Setting: n = 1000, d = 50, κ ∈ {10, 10², 10³, 10⁴, 10⁵}, covariance spectrum j^−α, α ∈ {0, 1, 2}, textbook step
γ = 1/(4R²), Δ = 3γ², 5 seeds, exact instrumentation every n/10 iterations.

**(a) Size of the gap.** The ratio between the exact one-step contraction exponent and the certified one
(−log ρ_exact / −log ρ_bound, averaged over passes 2–20) is 3.2–3.7 for κ ≤ 10² (memory-term regime of the
min, rate 1/(3n)), 5.1 (α = 0, 1) and 3.4 (α = 2) at κ = 10³, and then grows roughly like κ^0.9 in the
parameter-term regime: 29 / 16 / 6.0 at κ = 10⁴ and 220 / 100 / 19 at κ = 10⁵ for α = 0 / 1 / 2. Isotropic
problems (α = 0) show the largest gap. The late window (passes 20–100) gives the same picture (e.g. 187 / 72 / 11
at κ = 10⁵). Figure 1 shows the same gap in the trajectories: at κ = 10⁴, E‖θ_t−η*‖² decreases by 12 orders
of magnitude in 100 passes while the certified bound decreases by 2.

**(b) The exact chain reproduces the observed convergence.** The product of the exact conditional contractions
along one trajectory (violet in Figure 1) tracks the realized Lyapunov value and the mean over seeds: the
instrumentation is consistent and the whole gap is accounted for by the six inequalities.

**(c) Attribution.** "Share" of an inequality = λ_k / Σ_k λ_k with λ_k = log(ρ_{k−1}/ρ_k) the increment of the
contraction exponent (nats per iteration) when that inequality is undone, in the order B of the B3 note
(undo I5, then I4, I2, I3, I1 — the reverse of the proof order; the intermediate rates are the same as when
the inequalities are applied cumulatively in proof order, since the proof is a nested chain of substitutions).
Σ_k λ_k = (−log ρ_exact) − (−log ρ_bound) is the gap between the exact and the certified exponents; shares are
additive, not multiplicative (a share of 0.5 does not mean "a factor √speed-up").
* Memory-term regime (κ ≤ 10²): I1 (square norm) ≈ 0.39–0.44, I2 (cocoercivity) ≈ 0.32–0.38, I5 (min of the two
  rates) ≈ 0.07–0.22, I4 (strong convexity) ≈ 0.03–0.19, I3 (variance ≤ second moment) < 0.006.
* Parameter-term regime (κ ≥ 10⁴): I2 dominates (0.46 / 0.54–0.58 / 0.62–0.71 for α = 0 / 1 / 2), I4 is second
  (0.31–0.36 for all α at κ = 10⁵), I1 falls to 0.14 / 0.07 / 0.03, I5 to 0.06 / 0.02 / 0.01, I3 ≈ 0.
* At κ = 10³ with strong anisotropy (α = 2) cocoercivity alone carries 0.83 of the gap.
* Over the whole grid, I3 never exceeds 0.6 % and the min step I5 never exceeds 22 %.

**(d) Tightness ratios (geometric means over passes 2–20).**
* r_var ≥ 0.96 (≈ 0.99 typically): the variance–second-moment step is tight because the mean stored gradient
  z̄ is small compared with the memory error (‖z̄‖² ≪ M/n).
* r_sq ≈ 0.50 for κ ≤ 10²: exactly the factor 2 of ‖a+b‖² ≤ 2‖a‖² + 2‖b‖² (a and b are nearly uncorrelated);
  r_sq decreases to 0.075 / 0.015 / 0.0035 at κ = 10⁵ (α = 0 / 1 / 2): once the memory is fresh relative to the
  slow motion of θ, ω_t ≈ F′(θ_{t−1}) and the proof's bound on E‖ω_t‖² by individual deviations is very loose.
  Its share of the gap is nevertheless small at large κ because the γ²E‖ω_t‖² term is second order for
  γ = 1/(4R²).
* r_coco = L_eff/R² ∈ [0.16, 0.37] (α = 0), [0.11, 0.16] (α = 1), [0.019, 0.18] (α = 2): R² = max_i L_i
  overestimates the cocoercivity constant actually experienced by 3–50×; the loss is largest for anisotropic
  features, where ‖x_i‖² varies a lot across samples and the maximum is far from typical.
* r_sc = μ/μ_eff decreases from ≈ 0.9 (κ = 10) to 0.012 / 0.027 / 0.14 at κ = 10⁵ (α = 0 / 1 / 2), matching
  λ_min(F″(η*))/μ = 70 / 26 / 2.1: with anisotropic covariance the smallest Hessian eigenvalue stays close to λ, so
  strong convexity is nearly tight, and the gap moves entirely to cocoercivity.
* r_min ≥ 0.9998 and r_var ≥ 0.96 although I5 and I3 carry up to 22 % and 0.6 % of the gap: a ratio close to 1
  can still cost a visible fraction of a rate that is itself close to 1; the attribution chain (differences of
  contraction exponents) is the right measure, the ratios are the right diagnostic of *why*.
* r_init (I6, initialization) = 3·10⁻⁵–0.03: the Lipschitz bound at initialization is loose by 1.5–4.5 orders
  of magnitude, but it only affects the constant (1 + n/4) of the bound, not the rate.

**(e) Interpretation.** The proof loses little in its "stochastic" steps (I3, and I1 at large κ in terms of
impact) and much in the two steps where global worst-case constants replace local ones: cocoercivity with
R² = max_i L_i (I2) and strong convexity with μ = λ (I4). Which of the two dominates depends on the geometry:
isotropic or mildly anisotropic features → both (I2 ≈ 0.46–0.58, I4 ≈ 0.31–0.36); strongly anisotropic features
→ cocoercivity (0.62–0.83). The min step matters only in the memory-term regime κ ≲ n.

## 2. Hypothesis B2 — trajectory-dependent constants (Figure B2)

* μ ≤ μ_eff(θ_t) always (checked); along SAGA, μ_eff/μ starts at 1.8–50 (far from η*, where all directions
  contribute) and converges to λ_min(F″(η*))/μ when κ ≫ n: 9.94 vs 9.86 (α = 0, κ = 10⁴), 4.97 vs 4.97 (α = 1,
  κ = 10⁴), 1.47 vs 1.14 (α = 2, κ = 10⁴). For α ∈ {0, 1} the share of ‖θ_t−η*‖² carried by the single slowest
  eigenvector reaches 0.998–1.000 within ≈10 passes; for α = 2 it stays ≈ 0.002 because many eigenvalues of
  F″(η*) are close to λ (the slow subspace is multi-dimensional), even though μ_eff/μ is close to its minimum.
* Comparison with the predictions of the B2 note (Section "Direction dependence"): prediction (P1) for GD
  (μ_eff non-increasing, → λ_min(H*)) is confirmed in all configurations. Prediction (P2) for SAGA (error
  spread over the directions fed by the estimator noise, μ_eff fluctuating above λ_min(H*)) holds in the
  memory-term regime κ ≲ n, but NOT in the parameter-term regime κ ≫ n, where SAGA aligns with the slowest
  eigenvector as fast as or faster than GD (per pass). The note's quasi-stationary heuristic assumed that the
  noise covariance decays at the certified rate ρ_bound = 1 − 3μ/(16R²), slower than the slowest gradient-descent
  mode (1 − γλ_min(H*))²; in practice the noise decays at the observed rate (≈ 0.3 per pass at κ = 10⁴, α = 1),
  comparable to or faster than that mode (λ_min(H*)/(2R²) ≈ 0.25 per pass), so the gradient-descent term
  dominates and the error aligns — the theory-practice gap itself invalidates the pessimistic input of the
  heuristic, exactly the escape clause stated in the note ("a violation of (P2) would mean that the noise term
  decays faster than assumed").
* In the memory-term regime (κ ≲ n) the error does not align: at κ = 10² (α = 1) the share stays ≈ 0.001 and
  μ_eff/μ fluctuates between 1.5 and 5 (end value 2.15, geometric mean 1.9) while GD converges to 1.15; at
  κ = 10³ (α = 1) the share reaches only 0.31 after 60 passes. The effective number of error directions
  d_eff = 1/Σπ_k² (B2 note) makes this quantitative: SAGA ends at d_eff = 24 / 24 / 1.0 (α = 0, κ = 10², 10³, 10⁴)
  and 15 / 3.8 / 1.0 (α = 1), GD at 2.5 / 1.0 / 1.0 and 10 / 3.9 / 1.0; for α = 2 both stay spread
  (d_eff ≈ 12–15 at κ ≥ 10³) because the bottom of the spectrum of F″(η*) is nearly degenerate. Prediction (P1)
  (μ_eff non-increasing along GD) holds for 86–100 % of the recorded steps (exactly for a quadratic; the logistic
  Hessian varies along the path); the quadratic proxy Σ_k λ_k π_k(t) matches μ_eff within 1 % at the end of
  every run, so the direction of the error is the whole story near η*. This is consistent with Proposition 5.9: when 1/(3n) < 3μ/(16R²) all
  directions contract at the memory rate and no direction is "slow".
* L_eff/R² (geometric means along SAGA) ≈ 0.19–0.38 for α = 0, 0.11–0.16 for α = 1 and 0.012–0.09 for α = 2,
  with GD ending in the same ranges, all bracketed by the limits at η* (from 0.0026–0.19 to 0.21–0.53 depending on
  the direction): the effective cocoercivity constant is 3–100× smaller than R² and is essentially a property of
  the data (per-sample curvature σ′_i(η*)‖x_i‖² versus the maximum over samples), not of the algorithm.
* Consequence for the bound: replacing μ by μ_eff(θ_{t−1}) in the one-step inequality (a valid a-posteriori
  bound, see the B3 note) explains the I4 share (0.25–0.36 of the log-gap for κ ≥ 10⁴); replacing R² by
  L_eff(θ_{t−1}) explains the I2 share (0.46–0.71); together they account for 77–96 % of the log-gap for κ ≥ 10⁴
  (Figure 1, yellow curve), the remainder being the square-norm step and the min.

## 3. Hypothesis B1 — SAGA in a quadratic metric (Figure B1)

Setting: κ = 10³, α ∈ {1, 2}, P ∈ {I, diag(Σ̂)+λI, Σ̂+λI, F″(η*)}, step γ = 1/(4R_P²), 5 seeds.
* Empirical rate per pass of ‖θ_t−η*‖²_P: α = 1: 1.00 (I), 0.94 (diag), 1.11 (Σ̂+λI), 1.11 (F″); α = 2: 0.61 (I),
  0.27 (diag), 1.26 (Σ̂+λI), 1.24 (F″). The statistical and Hessian preconditioners reach F−F* = 10⁻¹⁶ in ≈25
  passes where SAGA is at 10⁻⁴·⁵; the diagonal preconditioner does not help (features are randomly rotated, so
  diag(Σ̂) is nearly uniform while the scaling changes the step) — Jacobi scaling is only useful for axis-aligned
  anisotropy.
* Transferred Proposition 5.9 with *global* constants (R_P² = max_i ‖x_i‖²_{P⁻¹}/4 + λλ_max(P⁻¹), μ_P = λλ_min(P⁻¹))
  predicts the opposite ordering: κ_P = 1000 (I), 1899 (diag), 5083 (Σ̂+λI), 3623 (F″) at α = 1, i.e. certified
  rates per pass 0.19, 0.10, 0.037, 0.052. The global constant μ_P is useless for a statistical preconditioner
  because λ_min(P⁻¹) = 1/(λ_max(Σ̂)+λ) is tiny.
* With the *local* constant μ_P = λ_min(F″(η*), P) (generalized eigenvalue) the certified rates become 0.33 (I),
  0.29 (diag), 0.333 (Σ̂+λI), 0.333 (F″) at α = 1: the preconditioned problems have local condition numbers 2.8
  and 1, and the bound saturates at the memory term n·1/(3n) = 1/3 per pass. The observed 1.1–1.26 is 3.4–3.8×
  larger: once the curvature is handled by P, the remaining looseness is in the memory part of the Lyapunov
  analysis (the coefficient 1 − 1/(3n) instead of the 1 − 1/n contraction of the memory itself, imposed by the
  choice Δ = 3γ² needed to absorb I1–I2).
* Tightness ratios in the P-metric: r_coco rises from 0.13 (I) to 0.33–0.34 (Σ̂+λI, F″) at α = 1 — the statistical
  metric homogenises the per-sample curvature (leverage scores) — while r_sc (with the global μ_P) falls to
  0.017–0.023: in the P-metric the gap moves from cocoercivity to the global strong-convexity constant, exactly
  as the B1 note predicts. r_var ≥ 0.96 in all metrics; r_sq is 0.38–0.53 except for P = I and diag at α = 2
  (0.21 and 0.09).

## 4. Constants inside the proof (Figure C)

n = 1000, κ = 10³, α = 1. Optimising (γ, Δ) within the proof structure gives γR² = 0.219, Δ = 2.56γ², certified
rate 2.19·10⁻⁴ per iteration vs 1.875·10⁻⁴ for the textbook constants: a gain of 17 %. SAGA in practice: rate per
pass 0.51 (γR² = 1/8), 0.97 (1/4, textbook), 1.14 (1/3), 1.09 (1/2), 0.95 (1), 0.66 (2), 0.027 (4). The
certified rate at the textbook step (0.22 per pass, Δ optimised) is 4.5× below the observed one, and SAGA remains
stable with steps 4–8× larger than the largest step the proof admits (γR² < 1/2). Conclusion: the constants of
Proposition 5.9 are within 17 % of the best the proof structure allows; the gap to practice is not a matter of
constants but of the inequalities I1, I2, I4.

## 5. Hypothesis A3 — the Lyapunov proof of L-Katyusha, instrumented (Figure A3)

Parameters of Theorem 4.6 of Kovalev–Horváth–Richtárik (θ₂ = 1/2, θ₁ = min{√(2σn/3), 1/2}, p = 1/n), exact
E[Ψ_{k+1} | state] by enumeration of the n indices, 3 seeds.
* Lemma 4.5 (E[Ψ_{k+1}] ≤ max{1/(1+ησ), 1−θ₁(1−θ₂), 1−pθ₁/(1+θ₁)} Ψ_k) is never violated (max of
  ρ_exact − ρ_lemma over all states: −1.7·10⁻³, −4.0·10⁻⁴, −2.8·10⁻⁴).
* The exact one-step contraction exponent exceeds the certified one by 4.7× (hard instance, κ = 10⁴), 2.8×
  (logistic κ = 10³, where the certified rate is the reference-update term pθ₁/(1+θ₁) = 1/(3n)) and 4.1×
  (logistic κ = 10⁴). The L-Katyusha analysis is thus loose by a constant factor only, much smaller than the
  κ-dependent gap of Proposition 5.9 on the same logistic problems (5–16×) — consistent with its optimal
  κ-dependence.
* The estimator-variance bound E‖g−F′(x)‖² ≤ 2L·D_F(w, x) has tightness r_var ≈ 0.13–0.15 (max 0.3–0.6): it loses
  the factor 7 that again comes from L = max_i L_i (expected smoothness would tighten it).
* Composition of Ψ: on logistic problems Y/Ψ ≈ 10⁻³ (the y-term is negligible), Z/Ψ and W/Ψ alternate with the
  reference updates; on the hard instance Z/Ψ ≈ 0.5–0.6 and W/Ψ ≈ 0.4–0.5.

## 6. Hypothesis A2 — naive acceleration versus the corrected coupling (Figure A2)

One seed; target ‖θ−η*‖² ≤ 10⁻¹⁰‖θ₀−η*‖² (trajectories) and 10⁻⁶ (grid); the naive scheme "Nesterov+SVRG"
is v = θ + β(θ − θ_prev), θ⁺ = v − γ g(v) with the loopless-SVRG estimator g (p = 1/n); "L-Katyusha without
negative momentum" is Algorithm 2 with θ₂ = 0 and η = 1/(3θ₁).
* Hard instance (n = 16, m = 64, κ = 10⁴): gradient evaluations to 10⁻¹⁰: AGD 5.3·10³, L-Katyusha 3.2·10⁴,
  best naive scheme 9.6·10⁴ (β = 0.95, γR² = 1/6), SAGA 4.3·10⁵, L-SVRG 1.9·10⁶; L-Katyusha without the
  θ₂w term diverges (‖θ−η*‖² ≈ 10²⁸ after 3.4·10³ gradients), and the naive scheme with the standard rule
  β = (√κ−1)/(√κ+1) diverges at γR² = 1/6 (it converges with 3× and 10× smaller steps, in 4.5·10³n and
  1.4·10⁴n gradients to 10⁻⁶, against 1.2·10³n for L-Katyusha with the paper's untuned parameters). Over the
  whole (β, γ) grid the best naive setting needs 3445n gradients to reach 10⁻⁶, 2.9× more than L-Katyusha.
* Logistic regression (n = 1000, d = 50, κ = 10⁴, α = 1): the picture is reversed. To 10⁻¹⁰: best naive scheme
  4.7·10⁴ (47 passes, β = 0.9, γR² = 1/6), L-Katyusha without negative momentum 4.9·10⁴, SAGA 8.8·10⁴,
  L-Katyusha 1.06·10⁵, L-SVRG 4.0·10⁵; AGD (step 1/L, κ_F = L/μ = 654) is still at 6·10⁻⁸ after 200 passes.
  To 10⁻⁶ the best naive setting needs 28n gradients against 56n for L-Katyusha; the √κ momentum rule again
  diverges at γR² = 1/6 and works at smaller steps (39n, 42n).
* Interpretation: the negative-momentum term is what makes a worst-case guarantee possible — without it the
  coupling diverges on the hard instance and the naive scheme must be detuned (smaller β or γ), losing the
  acceleration; on a benign logistic problem, whose effective curvature is far above μ (Section 2), the naive
  schemes work and even beat L-Katyusha run with the parameters prescribed by its theorem (which are tuned to
  the worst case: θ₁ = √(2n/(3κ)) ≈ 0.26). This is an empirical statement about these two instances; the
  κ-exponent of the naive scheme is the object of Section 7.

## 7. Hypothesis A4 — gradient complexity versus κ on the hard instances (Figures A4, A4b)

Target ‖θ−η*‖² ≤ 10⁻⁸‖θ₀−η*‖²; slopes = least-squares fit of log(gradients) against log κ over κ ≥ 10n;
n = 16 throughout. Runs that exhausted their wall-clock budget are excluded from the geometric means (listed
in the summaries); several of them are artefacts of the machine sleeping during the sweep and are being re-run.

**(a) Lan–Zhou normalisation (block-local regularizer, Nesterov chain ζ = 2, m = 256, κ = R²/μ solved exactly,
3 seeds; Figure A4).** Slopes: SAGA 1.02, L-SVRG 1.01, GD 1.03; L-Katyusha 0.52, AGD 0.55; naive Nesterov+SVRG
with the best (β, γ) of a grid selected on seed 0: 0.81; L-Katyusha without the negative-momentum term diverges
for every κ ≥ 10³ (it converges at κ = 10², 3·10²). The same picture on block quadratics with random spectra
(3 seeds, κ ≤ 10⁴ complete): SAGA 1.00, L-SVRG 1.00, GD 1.01, L-Katyusha 0.50, AGD 0.54, naive 0.83. Absolute
costs at κ = 10⁵ (hard instance): GD 8.2·10⁵, SAGA 3.3·10⁶, L-SVRG > 4·10⁶ (budget), naive 3.1·10⁵,
L-Katyusha 7.9·10⁴, AGD 1.3·10⁴.

**(b) The instance of the A1 note (shared regularizer, geometric chain ζ = ζ_Q, m = 256, 2 seeds; Figure A4b, top).**
Against the nominal κ: SAGA 0.99, GD 0.94, L-Katyusha 0.49, AGD 0.50; against κ_eff = R²/λ_min(F″): 1.02, 1.03,
0.53, 0.55. The window of the lower-bound argument ends at κ_max(m, ε) = n(16m²/log²(1/ε) − 1) + 1 ≈ 4.9·10⁴;
beyond it the chain is exhausted and κ_eff < κ (8.5·10⁴ at nominal 10⁵, 4.4·10⁵ at 10⁶), exactly as
Proposition "testable prediction" (iii) of the note states: the saturation is removed by plotting against κ_eff.

**(c) Aligned instance (all components equal to one chain, L_F = R², m = 2048, 2 seeds; Figure A4b, bottom).**
Slopes SAGA 1.00, L-Katyusha 0.50, AGD 0.51, GD 1.00, with the four orders separated as predicted by the note:
at κ = 10⁵ GD needs 1.1·10⁷ gradients, SAGA 2.75·10⁶, L-Katyusha 6.8·10⁴ and AGD 4.7·10⁴ (ratios GD/SAGA = 4.0,
SAGA/L-Katyusha = 40 ≈ √(κ/n)·1.3, AGD/L-Katyusha = 0.7).

**(d) Interpretation.**
* The exponents 1 (SAGA, L-SVRG) and 1/2 (L-Katyusha) of hypothesis (A4) are confirmed on three instances with
  2–3 seeds; the κ¹ exponent of SAGA is a theorem for constant steps (B3 note, Prop. "SAGA is not accelerated";
  A1 note, Corollary "SAGA sandwich"), the κ^½ exponent of L-Katyusha is certified optimal within the lower-bound
  window (A1 note).
* On the block-separable instances GD and AGD have the same exponents as SAGA and L-Katyusha with smaller
  constants, because L_F ≈ R²/n there (A1 note, Proposition "constants of the instance"): the proposal's
  expectation "AGD ∼ n√κ, GD ∼ nκ" holds only on aligned instances, where it is confirmed (c).
* The naive scheme, even re-tuned at every κ, has an intermediate exponent ≈ 0.8 on both block instances: tuning
  buys a constant but not the √κ scaling — consistent with the obstruction of the A3 note (closing the one-step
  inequality without the anchor costs a factor κ) and with its prediction (P3), and with the literature (no
  acceleration proof for b = 1). Removing the anchor from L-Katyusha itself is catastrophic (divergence) once
  κ ≥ 10³ ≈ 60n, matching prediction (P2) of the A3 note.

## 8. Hypothesis A3, additional ratios of the A3 note (Figure A3; 3 seeds)

* Centering factor r_cen = E‖g−F′(x)‖²/((1/n)Σ‖f′_i(x)−f′_i(w)‖²) ≈ 0.94–0.99: the per-sample gradient
  differences are not aligned, as expected for logistic regression and for the block instance.
* Co-coercivity factor (Bregman form) r_coco = (1/n)Σ‖f′_i(x)−f′_i(w)‖²/(2L·D_F(w,x)) ≈ 0.13–0.15: the whole
  slack of the variance bound (r_var = r_cen·r_coco ≈ 0.13–0.15) is the per-sample constant L = max_i L_i, the
  same mechanism as inequality I2 of Proposition 5.9.
* Cancellation shares: the signed share c(t) = ⟨F′(x_t), x_t−w_t⟩/D_F(w_t, x_t) of the Bregman gap removed by the
  anchor is negative on 64–77 % of the steps (median −1.5 on the hard instance, −0.6 on logistic regression) and
  above 1 on 8–20 %: the inner product cancelled in Step 6 of the proof is rarely a fraction in [0, 1] of the gap,
  which is why it has to be cancelled identically rather than bounded. Its size relative to the certified decrease,
  q(t) = |θ₂/θ₁ ⟨F′(x_t), x_t−w_t⟩|/((1−ρ)Ψ_t), has median 1.4 (hard instance, logistic κ = 10⁴) and 0.64
  (logistic κ = 10³), with q ≥ 1 on 65 % / 5 % of the steps: on the ill-conditioned problems the cancelled term is
  typically larger than the whole certified decrease, confirming prediction (P4) of the A3 note (it could not have
  been absorbed into ρΨ_t).
