## 2. The finite-sum problem: how the proof was found

1. First reduction (kept): the field is `E o(x + S)` with `S` the normalized sum; only an expansion with error
   `o(eps)`, `eps = b^{-1/2}`, uniformly in small `x`, is needed. For a lattice law this is a Riemann sum of
   `o(x+y) * (Edgeworth density)` over a lattice of mesh `eps`; the Riemann-sum error is governed by the singularity of `o`.
   Heuristic: error `eps^c` for a singular set of codimension `c`; `eps^1` with full oscillation for a rational
   hyperplane (sign); smaller than `eps` for a curved hypersurface.
2. FAILED route A — cite Bhattacharya–Rao Thm 20.8 (strongly non-lattice, `o(n^{-1/2})`) for the non-lattice case and
   Thm 22.1 (lattice local expansion) for the lattice case. Covers the two extremes but not mixed laws (closure of the
   group generated by the support = subspace + lattice), needs two different proofs, and gives no rate. Abandoned.
3. FAILED route B — Lindeberg swap against a Cramér law `Y` matching the moments of `zeta` up to order `m-1`, with the
   remainder `b * eps^m * E|D^m o_rho|` controlled by small-ball bounds (`rho^{c-m}`). Two obstacles: (i) a finitely
   supported `zeta` can sit on the boundary of the truncated moment cone, so no law with a density matches its moments
   (fixable by swapping blocks of `k >= m` samples, whose support is a simplex grid on which no polynomial of degree
   `< k` vanishes); (ii) the exponent bookkeeping only gives `o(eps)` for `c >= 2` with `m >= 5`, never the sharp
   `eps^c`, and nothing for `c = 1`. Abandoned for route C, but (i) is a usable trick.
4. Route C (kept, Section 3 of the note) — Fourier side. Key realisation: for any law,
   `int_{cube of side c/eps at t0} |phi_S(t)| dt <= C`, because `|phi|^2` is positive definite and a Fejér weight has a
   nonnegative transform, so the mass near any frequency is at most the mass near 0. Hence
   `|E F(x+S) - Edgeworth| <= C eps^3 |F|_1 + C sum_{j != 0} sup_{Q_j} |F^|`: the lattice case is the worst case, and
   the error is a lattice sum of `|F^|`. No arithmetic condition at all. Works verbatim for weighted sums, which merges
   batch size and momentum into one parameter `eps_w = max w / sqrt(rho_2)`.
5. Codimension >= 2: cut `o` at distance `eps` from the singular cone; `|D^m|_1 ~ eps^{c-m}`; small-ball bound from the
   ball-concentration inequality and a covering. Gives `eps^c` (logs).
6. Sharpness: exact computations (product-binomial lattice law) show a `b^{-1/2}` relative lattice term for `1x2`
   with three values according to `b mod 3`; FORMAL formula (regularized lattice sum of `y/|y|`) reproduces them:
   predicted -1.012 / +0.333 / -0.178, exact -1.045 (b=1026) / +0.337 (b=4096) / -0.179 (b=2048), drifting the right
   way. (A first evaluation was off by `sqrt 2` in the closed form of the Gaussian integral; fixed.)
7. Unexpected: for `2x3` (c = 2) the error is `O(1/b)`, not `b^{-1/2}`: the singular set is a curved variety, and the
   lattice term is averaged along it. CONJECTURE: for `K < d`, `K >= 2`, the lattice term is of smaller order than
   `b^{-(c+1)/2}`. Not investigated.

## 3. Square matrices

- `2x2`: `||M||_* = sqrt2 max(|y+|,|y-|)` with `y+-` the conformal / anticonformal parts; `V` is the cone over a Clifford
  torus; rotations of `y+` or `y-` alone are symmetries. This makes Fourier decay elementary (Bessel `J_0`, van der
  Corput on circles). PROVED: law holds for every bounded law, rate `eps^{8/7}` in the field.
  The exponent is not optimized: using both curved directions would give `|t|^{-2}` for the indicator and a better rate.
- NUMERICAL: transient locking. For the integer lattice through the origin (`b = 0 mod 3` in the example) the exact
  minimizer is 0 up to `b = 513`, then 0.031 (768), 0.051 (1026), target 0.114. Also locks at nonzero kinks: exactly 1/7 (b = 34, 64), 1/11 (b = 65). Singular integer matrices carry mass
  `~ log b / b` with a kink of the nuclear norm; drift is `~ b^{-1/2}`.
- `K = d >= 3`, CONJECTURE. What I have (not in the note in full):
  * sharp bounds `|D^k polar(M)| <= C_k sigma_{K-1}^{-k}` off `V`: spectral projectors of the Hermitian dilation via
    `P_{>c} = 1/2 + (1/2pi) PV int (H - c - is)^{-1} ds` with `c = (sigma_K + sigma_{K-1})/2`; the rank-one part
    `S = u_K v_K^T` through `S (x) S = P_u (x) P_v` and `DS = D(S (x) S) . S`. (A cruder contour gives an extra factor
    `sigma_1/sigma_{K-1}`; the Daleckii–Krein expansion has `1/sigma_K` terms that cancel in pairs.)
  * shape operator of `V` at a rank `K-1` point: swaps `u_i v_K^T <-> u_K v_i^T` with factor `-1/sigma_i`: `2(K-1)`
    nonzero curvatures `+-1/sigma_i`; tube map `(y, tau) -> y + tau n(y)`, `|tau| < sigma_{K-1}/2`, Jacobian in `[2^{-2(K-1)}, (3/2)^{2(K-1)}]`.
  * Fourier decay scheme: integrate by parts twice in the fixed direction `t/|t|` on `E \ V` (legitimate: `sigma_{K-1}^{-2}`
    integrable in `E`, `sigma_{K-1}^{-1}` on `V`); average the boundary integral over a Givens rotation of two rows
    (or columns) containing the largest row of `t`: phase `const + rho cos(theta - theta_0)`, `rho >= |<G^T t, y>|`.
    Reduces to a sublevel estimate `int_{V cap shell} (1 + 1/sigma_{K-1}) 1{|<lambda,y>| <= s} dS <= C s^gamma`, which
    I can get with `gamma = 1/(2K)` from the tube map + Pólya's inequality for `det` along a line in `lambda^perp`.
  * layer probability: majorant `Theta(det/r) chi`, rotation-invariant, so its transform is a `J_0`-average of a volume
    integral. MISSING: the bound must be linear in `r` with a gain in `|t|`; my estimate
    `vol{|det| <= r, |<lambda,y>| <= s} <= C r s^{2 gamma/(K+1)}` relies on Weyl-formula volume computations I did not
    check carefully. Also MISSING: the dyadic-shell argument near the stratum `sigma_{K-1} = 0` (for `K = 2` it is the
    origin and homogeneity does the work; for `K >= 3` one needs either polynomial control of all constants in
    `1/rho_0` or an induction on the rank drop).
  * FAILED idea: a soft `o(|t|^{-1})` decay (Dini argument on sublevel sets) is enough on one shell but not after summing
    `~ log log` shells; a rate is necessary.

## 4. Sign descent

Esseen's lattice expansion gives everything: median of `S_b` = point of `ba + hZ` nearest to `-kappa_3/(6 sigma^2)`.
Known ingredients (Esseen 1945; Buhler–Gamst–Graham–Hales 2017 for the sign of mean - median). The reading "sign
descent can be exactly unbiased, or oscillate for ever, while orthogonalization converges" is the point.

## 5. The sign of a_1

- FAILED: Hölder / log-convexity of moments gives only `D_3/D_1 <= K+d+1`, i.e. `q >= -1`. Reason found afterwards:
  the inequality is third order (both sides agree to second order in the dispersion of the size-biased spectrum).
- DISCOVERY: with `R(p) = E sum_j s_j^p A_j / E sum_j s_j^p = Kd + p - D_{p+2}/D_p`, one has `R(2) = (K-1)(d-1)`,
  `R(4) = (K-1)(d-1)(K+d)/(K+d+1)`, `R(1) = q + (K-1)(d-1)`, and `a_1 < 0` is exactly `2R(1) + R(4) > 3R(2)`.
  Equality for Gamma laws, i.e. `K = 1`.
- PROVED (agent, verified by me): `K = 2` by hand (`q_{2,d} = E[c (1+c)^{-1/2}] / E[(1+c)^{1/2}]`, `c ~ Beta(d-1,1)`);
  `K >= 3` by the Taylor minorant `T_5` (moments to `E tr W^6`) for large sizes and 4-node Hermite minorants (moments to
  `E tr W^8`) for 52 small sizes. My verification: independent Wick enumeration of `E tr W^k`, `k <= 8`
  (`checks/v01`), recomputation of `NUM` and of the positivity of the shifted coefficients (`checks/v02`), reading and
  rerunning `a1/finite.py`.
- DISPROVED: global concavity of `r(p) = D_{p+2}/D_p` (it has the affine asymptote `p + K + d - 1` from above at `p = 4`).
- CONJECTURES: `r` concave on `[0,4]` (74 sizes); `d ((K-1)/2 - q_{K,d})` decreasing in `d` with limit `(K-1)(K+2)/16`.
- NOT PURSUED: Gamma-mixture representation; five-term recurrence for real Wishart moments (Cunden et al., Cor. 8.4)
  at half-integer exponents (it couples `M(3/2), M(1/2), M(-1/2), M(-3/2), M(-5/2)`, three of them unknown).

## 6. Directions examined and not pursued

- Dynamics with state-dependent noise (Section 4 of the brief). The new expansions hold at a fixed point; uniformity in
  `W` for a finite sum needs the lemmas uniformly over a compact family of laws (constants depend only on `M`,
  `lambda_min(Sigma)`, so this looks routine for `c >= 2`), after which Theorem 12 of the first note localizes zeros.
  Convergence to them (non-gradient field, discontinuous for `c = 1`) was not attempted.
- Degenerate covariance (`n <= Kd`). The whole argument is on `x + E_zeta`; needs a uniform codimension-2 transversality
  of the rank-deficient variety with these affine subspaces, and `J > 0` on all of `E`. Rank-one per-sample gradients
  make `E_zeta` very special. Open and practically the most relevant gap.
- Sampling without replacement: characteristic function of a simple random sample (Erdős–Rényi / Bloznelis–Götze) —
  the positive-definiteness trick does not apply directly. Open.
- Higher-order hierarchy: for `c >= 3` the next term is `O(b^{-2})` and is a contraction of `K_5`, `K_3 (x) K_4`,
  `K_3^{(x)3}` with derivatives of `psi_Sigma` at 0; not computed.
