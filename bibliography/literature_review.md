**Notation.** F = (1/n) Σ_i f_i with each f_i convex and L_max-smooth (Bach's book writes R^2 for this per-component constant, e.g. features bounded by R in logistic regression), F μ-strongly convex, κ = L_max/μ (Bach: R^2/μ), η* the minimizer, L = L(F) the smoothness constant of the average, L̄ = (1/n) Σ_i L_i, d the dimension, ε the target accuracy.

## 1. Scope and reading guide

The project starts from Proposition 5.9 of [bach2024learning] (SAGA with γ = 1/(4R^2), Lyapunov function V_t = ‖θ_t − η*‖^2 + 3γ^2 Σ_i ‖z_i^(t) − f_i'(η*)‖^2, rate 1 − min{1/(3n), 3μ/(16R^2)}) and asks three families of questions: how far the (n + κ) log(1/ε) complexity of this proof is from the Ω((n + √(nκ)) log(1/ε)) lower bound and how the gap is closed by "corrected" momentum (A1–A4); what happens to each inequality of the proof when the Euclidean metric is replaced by a preconditioner P or when the global constants (R^2, μ) are replaced by trajectory-dependent ones (B1–B2); and whether an instrumented version of the proof on l2-regularized logistic regression can locate the loose inequality in each geometry (B3). The review is organised accordingly: Sections 2–3 cover the non-accelerated methods and the lower bounds, Section 4 acceleration, Section 5 preconditioning, Section 6 tight (computer-aided) analyses and effective curvature, Section 7 the geometry of logistic regression and benchmark design, Section 8 a hypothesis-by-hypothesis gap analysis, and Section 9 a reading order. All statements below are taken from the reference list; where the list does not contain a result, this is said explicitly rather than filled in.

## 2. Variance-reduced methods and their Lyapunov analyses

The tutorial [gower2020variance] and the survey [bottou2018optimization] (Section 5.3) give the control-variate view: SAG [leroux2012stochastic, schmidt2017minimizing] moves along a biased average of stored gradients (a randomised version of the deterministic IAG method of [blatt2007convergent], whose cyclic-order rate is only O(n^2 κ^2 log(1/ε)) [gurbuzbalaban2017convergence]); SVRG [johnson2013accelerating] uses a snapshot and an inner loop; SAGA [defazio2014saga] keeps one gradient per component and is unbiased; SDCA [shalevshwartz2013stochastic] works in the dual; Finito [defazio2014finito] and MISO [mairal2015incremental] store points or surrogates and need the "big-data condition" n ≥ 2κ; SARAH [nguyen2017sarah] uses a biased recursive estimator; the loopless versions [hofmann2015variance, kovalev2020dont] replace the inner loop by a coin flip. All obtain O((n + κ_max) log(1/ε)) component-gradient evaluations with κ_max = L_max/μ, versus O(nκ log(1/ε)) for gradient descent ("the product of n and κ is replaced by a sum", [bach2024learning]); [bottou2018optimization] stresses that this is only better than plain SGD in the regime κ ≳ n or for high accuracy.

### 2.2 Bach's Proposition 5.9 versus the SAGA paper, and versus practice

Three differences separate the two proofs of the same algorithm. (i) Step and constants: [defazio2014saga] uses γ = 1/(3L) and gets 1 − min{1/(4n), μ/(3L)} (or γ = 1/(2(μn + L)) and 1 − μ/(2(μn + L)) when each f_i is μ-strongly convex, degrading to 1 − μ/(6(μn + L)) when only F is); [bach2024learning] uses γ = 1/(4R^2) and gets 1 − min{1/(3n), 3μ/(16R^2)}, with the factor (1 + n/4) coming from the Lipschitz initialisation z_i^(0) = f_i'(θ_0). (ii) Lyapunov function: SAGA's T^k is a Bregman (function-value) potential, while Bach's V_t stores squared gradient errors with the weight Δ = 3γ^2; this gradient-memory family is also used by [schmidt2015nonuniform] (Prop. 1a: step 1/(4L + nμ), rate exactly 1 − 1/(n + 4κ)), [hofmann2015variance] (weight S = σγn/(Lq)), [kovalev2020dont] (4η^2/(pn)), [gorbunov2020unified] (Mγ^2 with M = 4n), [gower2021stochastic] (α/(2L_2)), [defazio2016simple] (c = 1/(μL)) and [hu2017unified] (p_1/p_2). PEPit [goujaud2024pepit] shows that the SAGA paper's one-step contraction is exactly tight for its Bregman Lyapunov (worst case 0.966667 = 1 − γμ at L = 1, μ = 0.1, n = 5), whereas no such certificate exists in the list for the gradient-memory family with Bach's weights; the LMI of [hu2017unified] is the closest tool and its authors report that the numerical SDP certifies sharper rates than their closed forms. (iii) The smoothness constant: for the logistic loss f_i is (‖x_i‖^2/4)-smooth, so if R bounds the features, R^2 ≥ 4L_max and γ = 1/(4R^2) ≤ 1/(16L_max) — the same 1/(16L) as [schmidt2017minimizing] and sixteen times smaller than the 1/L used in practice by SAG [schmidt2017minimizing] and SVRG [babanezhad2015stop]. The κ-term of Bach's complexity, 16R^2/(3μ) ≥ 64κ_max/3 ≈ 21κ_max, compares with 3κ_max [defazio2014saga], 4κ_max [schmidt2015nonuniform, gower2021stochastic], 5κ_max [hofmann2015variance], 6κ_max [gorbunov2020unified, kovalev2020dont] and 10κ_max [hu2017unified]; the n-terms range from exactly n [schmidt2015nonuniform, gower2021stochastic] through 2n [gorbunov2020unified, kovalev2020dont] and 3n [bach2024learning, hofmann2015variance, hu2017unified] to 4n [defazio2014saga].

Practical step sizes documented in the list: 1/L and 2/(L + nμ) for SAG "even though we do not have a formal analysis" [leroux2012stochastic, schmidt2017minimizing]; "chosen so as to give the fastest convergence" [defazio2014saga] and grid search over powers of 2 [defazio2016simple] (the thesis [defazio2014new] states that the most effective step "can be found by testing a number of step sizes" and that SVRG with m = n is far from its best practical performance); m = n, η = 1/L and the last iterate for SVRG [babanezhad2015stop], against the theory's Option II and m ≥ 20L_max/μ [johnson2013accelerating, sebbouh2019towards]; 0.1/L for SVRG, which on rcv1 "matches our theoretical analysis" [xiao2014proximal] and which [hannah2018breaking] explains by the long epochs (the small step is an artefact of m ≈ κ, not of the estimator); for SAGA, 1/L "did not appear to give better results" than the μ-free theoretical (2 − √2)/(4L) [hofmann2015variance]; the step rule of [gazagnadou2019optimal] performs like grid search for minibatches; [sebbouh2019towards] closes the SVRG gap by changing the Lyapunov function rather than the algorithm; the "Bottou trick" and stochastic line searches have no theory [mairal2015incremental, gower2020variance]. Two mechanisms produce most of the slack: the generalized square-norm inequality ‖a + b‖^2 ≤ (1 + β)‖a‖^2 + (1 + 1/β)‖b‖^2 used with β = 1 ("for the sake of simplicity, we sacrifice tightness", [hofmann2015variance]) and cocoercivity with the worst-case per-component constant (R^2 or L_max) instead of the expected-smoothness constant L_1 ≤ L_max [gower2021stochastic, gower2019sgd] or the average L̄ [needell2016stochastic, xiao2014proximal, schmidt2015nonuniform].

## 3. Lower bounds for finite sums

All finite-sum lower bounds in the list descend from two building blocks: the black-box oracle model and resisting-oracle technique of [nemirovsky1983problem], and Nesterov's chain quadratic ("worst function in the world", tridiagonal Hessian, minimizer x*_k = q^k with q = (√Q − 1)/(√Q + 1)) under the linear-span assumption, Theorems 2.1.7 and 2.1.13 of [nesterov2018lectures] (the same statements and numbering are in the 2004 edition [nesterov2004introductory]). The finite-sum constructions replicate the chain either once per component (variable partition: [agarwal2015lower, lan2018optimal, zhou2019lower, zhang2020tight, hendrikx2021optimal]) or by distributing the rows of the bidiagonal factor of the chain's Hessian over components (Hessian partition: [xie2019general, han2024lower]); the "zero-chain" vocabulary (one oracle call activates at most one new coordinate) and the random-rotation device that extends span-based arguments to arbitrary randomized algorithms are formalised in [carmon2020lower]. A second, structural route avoids oracles altogether: the p-SCLI/CLI framework of [arjevani2016lower] treats fixed-step methods on quadratics as linear recursions and bounds their rate by Chebyshev polynomials, which gives dimension-free bounds [arjevani2016iteration, arjevani2016dimension, arjevani2017limitations].

**Exact status of (A1).** The first sentence — span-based methods with i.i.d. uniform sampling need Ω((n + √(nκ)) log(1/ε)) — is established, with three caveats on what "span-based" and "i.i.d." buy. [lan2018optimal] proves it for any fixed sampling distribution and iterates in the span of the sampled component gradients, in a dimension that must grow with the iteration count (the ar5iv-style label "Theorem 3.2" sometimes quoted is not the arXiv numbering; it is Theorem 3, eqs. 3.42–3.44); [arjevani2016dimension] proves the same order dimension-free for oblivious algorithms, which include SAGA with the fixed step γ = 1/(4R^2) of Proposition 5.9; [xie2019general] and [han2024lower] reduce the dimension to O(√(κ/n) log(1/ε)) and add proximal oracles without changing the rate. The n log(1/ε) term is a coupon-collector effect of i.i.d. sampling combined with the span restriction: [hannah2018breaking] shows it is unavoidable for SAGA/SAG/SDCA/Finito-type methods when κ = O(n), while [agarwal2015lower] (deterministic index sequences) and [woodworth2016tight] (fully adaptive randomized sequences, no span) only have an additive n (the random-direction technique behind the no-span bounds is isolated in the note [woodworth2017lower]). The matching upper bound is Katyusha [allenzhu2018katyusha] (Theorem 2.1 / Corollary 3.2), and Point-SAGA shows it is attained even with the proximal oracle [defazio2016simple, xie2019general].

The second sentence must be reworded. The weakening of n log(1/ε) to n log(1/ε)/(1 + (log(n/κ))_+) when κ ≪ n is proved for algorithms that break the i.i.d.-span assumption by full-gradient (or sweep) steps: [hannah2018breaking] (Theorem 2 for Prox-SVRG/SARAH with epoch length m = n + 121Q, Theorem 4 for oblivious p-CLI algorithms, the speed-up 1 + (ln(n/κ))_+ being optimal), [han2024lower] (span-based PIFO methods with a fixed distribution plus loopless full-gradient coins, which is exactly the protocol of L-SVRG/L-Katyusha), and [zhang2020tight] (no predefined distribution, but under the explicit zero-chain property; unrefereed). For fully adaptive randomized algorithms without any span assumption, the √(nκ) log term survives [woodworth2016tight], but nothing beyond an additive n is proved, and [peng2026dense] shows the same "n log(1/ε)/(1 + log-factor)" structure for arbitrary history-dependent index sequences only under the Polyak–Łojasiewicz condition, with thresholds at κ ≈ √n. The recommended wording is therefore: "under i.i.d. sampling and the span assumption, Ω((n + √(nκ)) log(1/ε)); methods that may take full-gradient steps still need Ω(n + √(nκ) log(1/ε)) when κ ≳ n, while for κ ≪ n the n log(1/ε) term weakens to n log(1/ε)/(1 + log(n/κ)), which modified SVRG attains." Two further refinements matter for the project: individual smoothness is essential (under average smoothness the bound becomes n^{3/4}√κ [zhou2019lower, han2024lower, zhang2020tight], attained by KatyushaX), and knowledge of μ is essential for the √(nκ) rate (oblivious algorithms without μ cannot beat Ω̃(n + κ ln(1/ε)) [arjevani2017limitations]; acceleration needs time-varying, μ-dependent coefficients [arjevani2016iteration, arjevani2016lower]).

## 4. Acceleration of variance reduction

### 4.1 Why naive Nesterov + SVRG estimator is not known to accelerate

The classical analysis of SVRG bounds the second moment of the estimator by E‖v‖^2 ≤ 4L[f(x) − f* + f(w̃) − f*] [johnson2013accelerating, gower2020variance]. Plugging this estimator into Nesterov's scheme fails for a combination of reasons that the list documents from four angles. (a) Error accumulation of fast methods: with an inexact gradient the basic (proximal-)gradient method tolerates summable errors, but the accelerated method weights the error of iteration i by i, so the errors must decay like 1/k^{2+δ} [schmidt2011convergence] (Proposition 2); [devolder2014first] proves that this accumulation is unavoidable for every method with rate O(1/k^p), p > 1. A stochastic estimator whose variance is proportional to the suboptimality at the snapshot does not satisfy this unless the snapshot is refreshed often or the iterates are kept near it. (b) The tight variance bound E‖v − ∇f(x)‖^2 ≤ 2L(f(w̃) − f(x) − ⟨∇f(x), w̃ − x⟩) contains an inner-product term; Allen-Zhu explains (Section 1 and Remark 5.7 of [allenzhu2018katyusha]) that the loose 4L bound "is insufficient for acceleration" and that it is this inner product that an accelerated analysis cannot absorb; [zhou2019direct] states the same: "the major task of the negative momentum is to cancel the additional inner product term shown in the variance bound". (c) The concrete naive method, Acc-Prox-SVRG [nitanda2014stochastic], has complexity O((n + ((n − b)/(n − 1))κ) log(1/ε)) for small minibatches b and only O((n + b√κ) log(1/ε)) once b exceeds a threshold; [nitanda2016accelerated] rewrites this as O((n + nκ/(n + √κ)) log(1/ε)) = O((n + min{κ, n√κ}) log(1/ε)), so acceleration over SVRG appears only when √κ > n: the variance has to be beaten down by the minibatch, not absorbed by the analysis. [allenzhu2018katyusha] treats τ_2 = 0 (no negative momentum) as this method and shows in Figure 1 that it is no longer accelerated. (d) Momentum with stochastic gradients can fail even on linear regression: heavy-ball and Nesterov momentum cannot beat SGD on simple instances [kidambi2018insufficiency]; heavy-ball tuned for quadratics cycles on a strongly convex one-dimensional function [lessard2016analysis]; stationary/oblivious schemes whose coefficients do not depend on μ cannot accelerate [arjevani2016iteration, arjevani2017limitations]. The SDP viewpoint [hu2018dissipativity] makes the mechanism explicit: the Lyapunov certificate for Katyusha exists only when the storage function couples the mirror iterate with the reference point.

### 4.2 Negative momentum and reference points

Katyusha [allenzhu2018katyusha] adds to the linear-coupling form of Nesterov's method [allenzhu2017linear] (y = gradient step, z = mirror step, query point x = τ_1 z + (1 − τ_1 − τ_2) y) a third term τ_2 x̃ toward the SVRG snapshot ("Katyusha momentum", a "magnet" with τ_2 = 1/2 that keeps the query point close to the point where the estimator is exact). With τ_1 = min{√(mσ/(3L)), 1/2}, α = 1/(3τ_1 L), m = 2n, it attains O((n + √(nκ)) log(1/ε)) (Theorem 2.1, Corollary 3.2), matching the lower bounds of Section 3. MiG [zhou2018simple] drops Nesterov's momentum and keeps only the two-point coupling y = θx + (1 − θ)x̃ (θ = √(m/(3κ)), η = 1/(3θL) when m/κ ≤ 3/4; θ = 1/2, η = 2/(3L) otherwise) and still obtains O((n + √(κn)) log(1/ε)) — the first acceleration without Nesterov momentum; restarting each epoch from the snapshot instead of the last iterate degrades it to O((n + κ) log(1/ε)). SSNM [zhou2019direct] transfers the idea to SAGA, which has no snapshot: the negative momentum points to a randomly sampled stored point (double sampling), the proof uses SAGA's own Lyapunov function (table term + distance term, "the same as the one in SAGA"), with E[T^{k+1}] ≤ (1 + ημ)^{-1}T^k, η = √(1/(3μnL)) when n/κ ≤ 3/4 and η = 1/(2μn) otherwise. [driggs2022accelerating] explains why the reference point is estimator-specific (it needs a point where the full gradient is known) and quantifies what is lost without it: their universal momentum framework accelerates SAGA/SVRG/SARAH/SARGE in κ, but the n-dependence degrades from √n to n^{2/3}.

The loopless L-Katyusha [kovalev2020dont] is the version the project reproduces. With σ = μ/L, θ_1 = min{√(2σn/3), 1/2}, θ_2 = 1/2, p = 1/n and η = θ_2/((1 + θ_2)θ_1), the Lyapunov function Ψ = Z + Y + W (Z = (L(1 + ησ)/(2η))‖z − x*‖^2, Y = (1/θ_1)(f(y) − f*), W = (θ_2(1 + θ_1)/(pθ_1))(f(w) − f*)) satisfies E[Ψ^{k+1}] ≤ (1/(1 + ησ))Z^k + (1 − θ_1(1 − θ_2))Y^k + (1 − pθ_1/(1 + θ_1))W^k ≤ max{1/(1 + ησ), 1 − θ_1(1 − θ_2), 1 − pθ_1/(1 + θ_1)}Ψ^k (Lemma 10 of [kovalev2020dont], eq. (18)), hence E[Ψ^{k+1}] ≤ (1 − θ)Ψ^k with θ = min{σ/(6θ_1), θ_1/(2n)} and O((n + √(nκ)) log(1/ε)) iterations (Theorem 11 and its proof; Lemma 4.5 and Theorem 4.6 in the arXiv version 1901.08689; the second term of the min, θ_1/(2n), on which two earlier transcriptions disagreed, has been re-read in the PMLR text). The W-recursion E[W^{k+1}] = (1 − p)W^k + (…)Y^k is the loopless analogue of the (1 − 1/n) table-update step of Proposition 5.9, and the L-SVRG Lyapunov Φ^k = ‖x^k − x*‖^2 + (4η^2/(pn))Σ_i‖∇f_i(w^k) − ∇f_i(x*)‖^2 is literally Bach's V_t with a coin-flipped table, so Lemmas 1–4 of [kovalev2020dont] (Lemmas 3.1–3.4 in the arXiv version; descent, variance bound, memory recursion, combination) map one-to-one onto the inequality chain of Bach's proof. [qian2021lsvrg] abstracts the variance information of the same Ψ into a single expected-smoothness constant L_2 (complexity O((1/p + √(L_f/μ) + √(L_2/(μp))) log(1/ε))), [kulunchakov2019estimate, kulunchakov2020estimate] obtain the same rate through estimate sequences with an anchor updated with probability 1/n, [li2021anita] (ANITA, unpublished) with a two-term potential f(w_t) − f* + ((1 + μη)pθ/(2η))‖x_t − x*‖^2 contracting at 1 − 4pθ/5, and [song2020variance] (VRADA) with a generalised estimate sequence that also improves the n log n term to n log log n; [bansal2019potential] is the pedagogical template for potential-function proofs of acceleration (A_k(f(x_k) − f*) + (L/2)‖z_k − x*‖^2).

### 4.3 Catalyst-type and other routes

Indirect acceleration wraps any linearly convergent method in an inexact accelerated proximal-point loop: Catalyst [lin2015universal, lin2018catalyst] and APPA [frostig2015unregularizing] turn O((n + κ) log(1/ε)) into Õ((n + √(nκ)) log(1/ε)), at the price of logarithmic factors in κ and of warm-start/stopping rules; the authors note that "for all incremental algorithms, there is indeed no acceleration" when n ≥ L/μ, and that SAGA without acceleration can win because of local strong convexity (the ocr experiment). The accelerated SDCA of [shalevshwartz2014accelerated] is the historical first O(n + √(nκ)) method and has the same inner–outer structure. Direct methods that do not use a negative-momentum term exist and are optimal: APCG [lin2015accelerated] and SPDC [zhang2015stochastic] accelerate in the dual or primal–dual (the per-coordinate dual step is exact, so no variance has to be absorbed), RPDG [lan2018optimal] and RGEM [lan2018random] use a game/extrapolation formulation (RGEM never computes a full gradient), Varag [lan2019unified] unifies the convex and strongly convex cases with one step-size policy and an explicit regime split at m = 3L/(4μ), and Point-SAGA [defazio2016simple] accelerates with an implicit (proximal) step and no momentum at all, using Bach-type Lyapunov with weight c = 1/(μL); [lan2020first] is the textbook source for RPDG and the lower bound in one notation. AIDE [reddi2016aide] is the Catalyst route applied to statistically preconditioned DANE (Section 5).

### 4.4 What is known empirically about κ-scaling

The only paper in the list that states an empirical exponent is [zhou2019direct]: on a9a (λ = 10^{-6}, 10^{-7}) and covtype (λ = 10^{-8}, 10^{-9}) "around 3 times slow-down when κ is 10 times larger … approximately √10 times slow-down … is recorded for all the accelerated methods. In comparison, SAGA shows significant slow-down", i.e. the κ^{1/2} versus κ^1 exponents of (A4), but measured on two λ values per dataset and on real data, not on a hard instance. [allenzhu2018katyusha] sweeps λ ∈ {10^{-4}, 10^{-6}, 10^{-7}} and shows the τ_2 = 0 ablation; [zhou2018simple] sweeps 10^{-4} … 10^{-8}; [kulunchakov2019estimate] compares λ = 1/(10n) and 1/(100n), finds acceleration more effective at the smaller λ, and attributes the absence of gain on one data set (alpha) to "hidden strong convexity"; [lin2018catalyst] and [lin2015universal] report gains only on ill-conditioned problems; [song2020variance] finds plain SVRG competitive with accelerated methods at λ = 10^{-4} and attributes it to [hannah2018breaking]; [kovalev2020dont] sweeps μ but deliberately does not compare with SAGA. No paper in the list fits exponents over a controlled κ sweep on a lower-bound instance, which is what (A4) proposes. Two negative results frame the scope: variance reduction is ineffective for deep networks [defazio2019ineffectiveness], and momentum's practical gains with plain stochastic gradients are "a by-product of mini-batching" [kidambi2018insufficiency].

## 5. Preconditioned / variable-metric variance reduction and statistical preconditioning

### 5.1 What replaces L and μ under a metric P

For a fixed symmetric positive definite P, the update θ_t = θ_{t−1} − γP^{-1}g_t is a Bregman (mirror) step with reference function h(θ) = (1/2)θ^T Pθ, and the right constants are those of relative smoothness and relative strong convexity [lu2018relatively, bauschke2017descent]: f is L_P-smooth and μ_P-strongly convex relative to h iff μ_P ∇^2h ≼ ∇^2f ≼ L_P ∇^2h, i.e. L_P = λ_max(P^{-1/2}∇^2f P^{-1/2}) and μ_P = λ_min(P^{-1/2}∇^2F P^{-1/2}) (for twice-differentiable f). Because h is quadratic, the change of variables θ ↦ P^{1/2}θ turns the preconditioned method into the Euclidean method on the transformed functions f_i(P^{-1/2}·), so every Euclidean proof transfers verbatim with R^2 → L_P := max_i λ_max(P^{-1/2}∇^2f_i P^{-1/2}) and μ → μ_P; this is the argument of [kolte2015accelerating] (SVRG on H^{-1/2}-transformed functions) and [gonen2016solving] (preconditioned data P^{-1/2}x_i). The sharpest finite-sum statement is Corollary 1 of [dragomir2021fast] (Bregman-SAGA with quadratic h): step 1/(8L_{f/h}), potential Ψ_t = (1/η)D_h(x*, x_t) + 2H_t with H_t = (1/n)Σ_i D_{f_i}(φ_i^t, x*), and E[Ψ_t] ≤ (1 − min{1/(2n), 1/(8κ_{f/h})})^tΨ_0 with κ_{f/h} = L_{f/h}/μ_{f/h} — to be set side by side with Proposition 5.9 (γ = 1/(4R^2), 1 − min{1/(3n), 3μ/(16R^2)}). For non-quadratic h the square-norm and "variance ≤ second moment" steps break because D_h is not translation invariant; the paper repairs this with a gain function G_t (Assumption 3), and [dragomir2022optimal] proves by a Bregman PEP that under relative smoothness alone no acceleration is possible, so "preconditioned acceleration" is a quadratic-metric phenomenon unless extra Hessian regularity is available (Lemma 2 of [hendrikx2020statistically]). [hanzely2021fastest] had left variance reduction under relative smoothness as an open question, answered by [dragomir2021fast] and, for the proximal-point variant, by [traore2025bregman] (unpublished); [hendrikx2020dual] gives a Bach-group example of a variance-reduced proof written with directional relative smoothness constants L_rel^i, the per-component analogue of R^2 in a non-Euclidean metric.

The preconditioned analyses with explicit SAGA/SVRG/Katyusha statements are [liu2019acceleration] (iPreSVRG and iPreKatX: the rates of SVRG and Katyusha X with κ replaced by κ_f^M = L_f^M/μ_f^M under ‖·‖_M, inexact M-prox solved by p = O(√(κ(M)) log(...)) FISTA steps, gains of order κ_f/√n and √κ_f/n^{1/4} when √n < κ_f < n^2/d^2) and PROMISE [frangella2024promise] (SketchySVRG/SAGA/Katyusha with a lazily updated rank-r Nyström or subsampled-Newton preconditioner): the constants replacing R^2 and μ are the quadratic-regularity constants γ_u, γ_l of F relative to the Hessian at a reference point (ratio 1 for quadratics, → 1 near the optimum with Lipschitz Hessians), inherited from the relative smoothness/convexity w.r.t. the Hessian of RSN [gower2019rsn] (rate (1 − ρμ̂/L̂)^k). Proposition 16 of [frangella2024promise] is the preconditioned version of the cocoercivity/variance step: E‖∇F_{b}(w) − ∇F_{b}(w')‖^2_{P^{-1}} ≤ 2L_P D_F(w, w') with L_P = [n(b − 1)/(b(n − 1))]γ_u + [(n − b)/(b(n − 1))]γ_u^max τ(1 + ζ), where τ is the Hessian dissimilarity max_i‖(H + νI)^{-1/2}(∇^2f_i + νI)(H + νI)^{-1/2}‖ [frangella2024sketchysgd]; with b = 1 (SAGA as in Bach's book) the term τ survives even for a perfect preconditioner, which identifies the per-component cocoercivity step, not the strong-convexity step, as the one that stays loose under preconditioning. For generalized linear models τ ≤ 1 + χ d_eff with χ the ridge-leverage coherence and d_eff = tr(H(H + νI)^{-1}) (Proposition 15 of [frangella2024promise], Proposition 4.7 of [frangella2024sketchysgd]); SketchySVRG then needs 10(n + 19b_g q̄/(1 − ζ)) log(1/ε) gradients, κ-free, and ridge regression O((n + χd_eff) log(1/ε)); the composite extension SAPPHIRE [sun2025sapphire] (unpublished) uses the scaled proximal map in the P-metric.

### 5.2 Leverage scores and effective dimension as the condition number after preconditioning

Several papers make precise that after whitening the remaining difficulty is coherence. With P = C + λI, the average condition number tr(C + λI)/λ_min(C + λI) that governs prox-SVRG with smoothness-proportional sampling becomes d, and with uniform sampling the constant is max_i‖P^{-1/2}x_i‖^2, i.e. n times the largest ridge leverage score [gonen2016solving] (a sketched rank-k Lanczos preconditioner gives κ̃ = (kλ_k + Σ_{i>k}λ_i)/λ at O(ndk log n) preprocessing). Leverage-score sampling replaces the √(nκ_sum) term of accelerated least-squares solvers by √(dκ_sum), since Σ_i‖a_i‖√τ_i ≤ √(d tr(A^TA)) [agarwal2020leverage]; a subsampled-Hessian preconditioner is spectrally accurate once the local sample size exceeds 3μ_coh d log(md/δ)/η^2 [wang2018giant]; Nyström PCG reaches a preconditioned condition number below 28 in expectation as soon as the rank is 2⌈1.5 d_eff(μ)⌉ + 1 [frangella2023randomized]; SVRN removes the κ-dependence of least squares by leverage-score sampling or a randomized Hadamard transform [derezinski2025stochastic]; pwSGD turns the condition number of l_p regression into a function of d only through a well-conditioned basis [yang2018weighted]; [wang2019utilizing] ties minibatch size and preconditioned condition number to d_λ and statistical leverage. The statistical-preconditioning line (P = local Hessian plus μ̃I) gives the sequence of rates κ^2/n [shamir2014communication] (DANE; its averaging step is the loose inequality), 1 + 2δ/μ [yuan2020convergence] (DANE-LS, δ = L√(32 log(p/δ')/n)), √(1 + 2μ̃/λ) [zhang2015disco] (DiSCO, self-concordant losses), κ_{F/φ} = 1 + 2μ̃/σ_F with μ̃ = O(R^2/√n) and accelerated √κ_{F/φ} [hendrikx2020statistically] (SPAG), the Catalyst-accelerated AIDE [reddi2016aide], and the optimal deterministic √(δ/μ) [kovalev2022optimal] matching the communication lower bound Ω(√(δ/λ) log(1/ε)) of [arjevani2015communication]; with variance reduction and acceleration the stochastic analogue is Õ(n + n^{3/4}√(δ/μ)) with a matching lower bound [lin2023stochastic].

### 5.3 Analyses that only use eigenvalue bounds, and cost per iteration

The quasi-Newton metrics — SQN [byrd2016stochastic], SLBFGS [moritz2016linearly], stochastic block BFGS [gower2016stochastic], VITE [lucchi2015variance] — and the diagonal Hutchinson preconditioner of Scaled SARAH / Scaled L-SVRG [sadiev2024stochastic] are analysed only through γI ≼ H ≼ ΓI: the proofs keep the Euclidean smoothness and variance inequalities and pay Γ/γ-type factors (exponential in d + M for SLBFGS, up to d for the diagonal case, where Appendix F.1 of [sadiev2024stochastic] argues the gap is unavoidable with current techniques), so the theoretical rate is worse than the Euclidean one even when practice improves. This is exactly the "loose step" of (B1): the proof never uses the fact that H approximates the Hessian. The opposite end is incremental Newton: IQN [mokhtari2018iqn] and NIM [rodomanov2016superlinearly] keep per-component quadratic models with cyclic updates and converge superlinearly at O(d^2) per iteration and O(nd^2) or O(nd + d^2) memory; SDNA [qu2016sdna] uses exact curvature of the sampled dual block; AdaSVRG [duboistaine2022svrg], AdaLVR [batardiere2023finite] and online scaling [zhang2026stochastic] adapt a scalar/diagonal/low-rank metric without knowing L (convex rates only). Costs per iteration: O(d) for diagonal P, O(rd) for low-rank-plus-identity (Nyström, truncated SVD) [kolte2015accelerating, frangella2024promise], O(Md) for L-BFGS two-loop recursions, O(d^2) for a dense P after an O(d^3) factorisation [derezinski2025stochastic], or O(d) after folding P^{-1/2} into the data once [gonen2016solving]; [liu2019acceleration] shows the O(d^2) cost of a dense M caps the speed-up when κ_f > n^2/d^2. Finally, two lower bounds bound the ambition of (B1): even with component Hessians and linear-algebraic operations on up to n/2 of them, Ω(n + √(nκ) log(1/ε)) oracle calls remain necessary in the worst case [arjevani2017oracle], and proximal oracles on smooth components do not change the finite-sum rate [han2024lower]; preconditioning can only change constants (κ → κ_P, L_max → leverage-type quantities) or exploit favourable geometries, which is where (B3) comes in.

## 6. Tight analyses and trajectory-dependent constants

### 6.1 PEP, IQC and SDP certificates for SAGA, SVRG and Katyusha

The performance-estimation problem of [drori2014performance] turns "the worst case of a method after N steps" into a convex SDP; the interpolation conditions of [taylor2017smooth] make it exact and dimension-free for L-smooth μ-strongly convex classes (a finite set of (x_i, g_i, f_i) is interpolable iff f_i ≥ f_j + ⟨g_j, x_i − x_j⟩ + (1/(2(1 − μ/L)))[(1/L)‖g_i − g_j‖^2 + μ‖x_i − x_j‖^2 − (2μ/L)⟨g_j − g_i, x_j − x_i⟩]); [lessard2016analysis] imports integral quadratic constraints from control (a small LMI certifies ‖x_k − x*‖ ≤ √cond(P) ρ^k‖x_0 − x*‖); [taylor2018lyapunov] combines the two to compute the best rate certifiable by a quadratic Lyapunov function with a given state, and [taylor2019stochastic] extends the design of potential functions to stochastic methods (unified variance model, over-parametrised models, coordinate descent), with the finite-sum case explicitly "not presented". For variance reduction specifically, [hu2017unified] models SAGA, SAG, Finito and SDCA as Markov jump linear systems and reduces the certificate to a 3 × 3 LMI whose Lyapunov function E[‖x − x*‖^2 + (p_1/p_2)Σ_i‖y_i − ∇f_i(x*)‖^2] has exactly the structure of Bach's V_t; [hu2018dissipativity] does the same for SVRG and Katyusha with dissipativity "supply rates" (recovering Johnson–Zhang's Theorem 1 exactly and Katyusha's parameters); [hu2021analysis] handles biased SGD with sequential SDPs. [goujaud2024pepit] (PEPit) provides the software: its SAGA example certifies Theorem 1 of [defazio2014saga] as tight for the Bregman Lyapunov (0.966667 = 1 − γμ), and its Point-SAGA example finds the bound of [defazio2016simple] slightly loose (0.971405 versus 0.973292). [goujaud2023fundamental] explains the structure that makes this useful for (B3): any worst-case proof is a nonnegative combination of interpolation inequalities, the SDP dual multipliers say which inequalities are active, and a Lyapunov sequence is obtained from the same mechanism; [upadhyaya2024automated, upadhyaya2025autolyap] automate "tight Lyapunov analysis" for deterministic finite-sum methods (no sampling), and [bousselmi2024interpolation] adds interpolation conditions for linear operators with prescribed spectra, which is what a worst case over all admissible preconditioners P needs.

### 6.2 Expected smoothness and data-dependent constants

The one-point inequality E_i‖∇f_i(x) − ∇f_i(x*)‖^2 ≤ 2L_cal(F(x) − F*) ("expected smoothness") [gower2019sgd, gower2021stochastic] is the exact data-dependent replacement of the cocoercivity step (5.31) of Proposition 5.9, and L_cal ≤ L_max can be far smaller under anisotropy; [needell2016stochastic] shows the hierarchy L ≤ L̄ ≤ L_max for SGD with (partially biased) importance sampling, [sebbouh2019towards] adds the expected residual ρ, and [gazagnadou2019optimal] gives computable bounds L_simple(b), L_practical(b) interpolating between L_max (b = 1) and L (b = n). Under quasi-strong convexity (a one-point condition at x*) the same proofs go through [gower2019sgd], which is the technical bridge to (B2).

### 6.3 Local strong convexity, restricted conditions and μ_eff(t)

The quantity μ_eff(t) = (θ_t − η*)^T F'(θ_t)/‖θ_t − η*‖^2 of (B2) is the restricted secant inequality constant of [zhang2013gradient] evaluated along the trajectory; [karimi2016linear] proves the chain SC ⇒ ESC ⇒ WSC ⇒ RSI ⇒ EB ⇔ PL ⇒ QG (Theorem 2; for convex functions RSI, EB, PL and QG are equivalent) and analyses SVRG under PL/QG with the Johnson–Zhang constant, [necoara2019linear] gives projected-gradient rates under quasi-strong convexity and quadratic growth (e.g. ‖x_k − x̄_k‖^2 ≤ (1 + κ_f/L_f)^{−k}‖x_0 − x̄_0‖^2 with step 1/L_f under quadratic growth with constant κ_f, Theorem 12), [agarwal2012fast] and [qu2016linear] use restricted strong convexity for M-estimators and SVRG, and [hu2017unified] shows that the SAGA certificate only needs the sum to satisfy the one-point sector condition S(m, L) at x* (the components keep their smoothness, and possibly convexity, assumptions), so Bach's strong-convexity step, itself a one-point inequality at η*, can be replaced by μ_eff. Two caveats: one-point conditions alone do not allow acceleration — gradient descent with step μ/L^2 is optimal on RSI(μ) ∩ EB(L) with rate (1 − μ^2/L^2)^k [guilleescuret2022gradient] — and oblivious algorithms need μ explicitly to accelerate [arjevani2017limitations], so a μ_eff-based accelerated rate requires convexity of the f_i and an estimate of μ_eff; [guilleescuret2021study] argues for reporting continuous, one-point condition numbers rather than L/μ, and [fercoq2019adaptive] and [roulet2020sharpness] give restart schemes that estimate the local growth constant online.

For logistic regression the local curvature is quantified by self-concordance: [bach2010self] (|φ'''| ≤ φ'' gives e^{−R‖v‖}F''(w) ≼ F''(w + v) ≼ e^{R‖v‖}F''(w), so within ‖θ − η*‖ ≲ 1/R the effective strong convexity is λ_min(H(η*)) + λ rather than λ), [bach2014adaptivity] (averaged SGD adapts to the lowest eigenvalue of the Hessian at the optimum without knowing it), [sun2019generalized] (the finite sum is (max_i‖x_i‖, 2)-generalized self-concordant, which bounds how fast μ_eff can drift along a trajectory), [ostrovskii2021finitesample] (Dikin ellipsoid, curvature parameter ρ with Σ ≼ ρH, ρ ≲ 1 + ‖θ*‖_Σ^3 for Gaussian designs), [marteauferey2019beyond] (everything is governed by H(θ*) and the degrees of freedom df_λ) and [freund2018condition] (λ_min(H(β*)) ≥ (λ_min(X^TX)/(4n)) exp(−ln2 ‖X‖/DegNSEP), a regularization-free curvature bound in terms of the degree of non-separability). [poon2018local] shows that SAGA/Prox-SVRG identify an active manifold in finite time and then run at a local rate, [frangella2024promise] measures a quadratic-regularity ratio that tends to 1 near the optimum, [pedregosa2020acceleration] shows that typical (average-case) rates depend on the whole Hessian spectral density rather than on κ alone, and [hofmann2015variance] discusses the transient phase. Published observations of the gap in the VR literature are consistent with this: for SAG the step 1/L "always converged and often performed better than the step size of 1/16L suggested by our analysis" [schmidt2017minimizing, Section 4.5], plain SAGA beats Catalyst-SAGA on ocr, and "one reason may be its ability to adapt to the unknown strong convexity parameter μ' ≥ μ of the objective near the solution" [lin2015universal], acceleration brings no gain on the alpha data set, where all SVRG-like methods "perform already well", which "may be due to hidden strong convexity" [kulunchakov2019estimate], accelerated methods look worse in the first passes on a9a, which the authors conjecture is "because the objective is locally well-conditioned around the initial point" [zhou2019direct], SAGA converges linearly under a growth condition without strong convexity [qian2019saga], and the step-size catalogue of Section 2.2 quantifies the factor-16 slack. What the list does not contain is any paper that measures μ_eff(t) or per-inequality slack along SAGA trajectories: (B2) and (B3) are, to the extent of this list, new.

## 7. Geometry of logistic regression and benchmark design

**Constants.** For f_i(θ) = log(1 + exp(−y_i x_i^Tθ)) + (λ/2)‖θ‖^2, L_i = ‖x_i‖^2/4 + λ, L_max = max_i‖x_i‖^2/4 + λ, L̄ = (1/n)Σ_i‖x_i‖^2/4 + λ, L(F) = λ_max((1/(4n))X^TX) + λ, and the Hessian is H(θ) = (1/n)X^TDX + λI with D_ii = σ(m_i)σ(−m_i) ≤ 1/4, m_i = y_i x_i^Tθ [boyd2004convex, lin2008trust]. Bach's R^2-smoothness of each f_i (features bounded by R) therefore overestimates L_max by a factor 4, and μ = λ is the only global strong-convexity constant, while the local curvature at η* is λ_min(H(η*)) + λ, with H(η*) a weighted covariance whose weights fall below 1/4 as the classifier becomes confident [liao2021hessian, bach2010self]. The dataset-dependent ratio L_max/λ_max((1/(4n))X^TX) (sample-norm anisotropy) and the covariance anisotropy (spectrum of Σ) are the two geometric knobs of (B3); the line-search estimate of L in the SAG code versus 0.25 max‖a_i‖^2 is a first "tightness ratio" of the smoothness inequality [schmidt2017minimizing].

**Hessian spectra.** For Gaussian isotropic features with c = d/n the empirical covariance follows the Marchenko–Pastur law with edges (1 ± √c)^2 [marchenko1967distribution], so the logistic Hessian at θ = 0 is MP/4 + λI and its condition number ((1 + √c)^2/4 + λ)/((1 − √c)^2/4 + λ) is set by c, not by λ, unless d/n is small; square Gaussian matrices have condition number of order n [edelman1988eigenvalues]. Anisotropic covariances (e.g. power-law spectra λ_j ∝ j^{−α} as in the synthetic protocol of [dieuleveut2017harder]: d = 25, eigenvalues 1/i^3, R^2 = trΣ, step 1/R^2) are handled by the Silverstein fixed-point equation [silverstein1995strong], and [liao2021hessian] characterises the logistic Hessian at any w: a deformed MP bulk shrunk by the weights g_i ≤ 1/4, multi-bulk spectra for heterogeneous Σ, and isolated eigenvalues created by a mean shift or by the signal (at most one due to w, possibly left of the bulk). The empirical regularized Hessian is a (1 ± δ_s)-copy of the population one once n ≳ 6ρ_λ^2 d_{1,λ} log d̃_{1,λ} with d_{1,λ} = Σ_j λ_j/(λ_j + λ) the effective dimension [hsu2014random], so the population spectrum can be prescribed. In the proportional regime the norm and local curvature of the l2-regularized optimum are explicit functions of (d/n, signal, λ) [salehi2019impact, mai2019large]: as λ → 0 the optimum inflates and the curvature collapses; for Gaussian designs the curvature parameter grows like 1 + ‖θ*‖_Σ^3 [ostrovskii2021finitesample].

**λ scaling and the two κ regimes.** Statistically motivated regularization lies between λ ≈ 1/√n and λ ≈ 1/n (fast rates O(1/n) for strongly convex regularized objectives [sridharan2008fast], source/capacity conditions [marteauferey2019beyond], "λ of order 1/√n or 1/n so κ of order √n or n" [zhang2015stochastic]); Bach's own experiment uses λ = R^2/n, i.e. κ = n, exactly the boundary where n + √(nκ) = 2n and acceleration cannot help [bach2024learning, lan2019unified, song2020variance]. The κ ≫ n regime of (A3)/(A4) therefore requires λ ≪ R^2/n and must be presented as an optimization regime, with the proviso that [hannah2018breaking] predicts a log(n/κ) advantage for SVRG-type methods in the opposite regime. In the historical VR papers λn is of order 1–10 [johnson2013accelerating, schmidt2017minimizing], while the acceleration papers push λ to 10^{-6}–10^{-9} to make √(nκ) visible [allenzhu2018katyusha, zhou2018simple, zhou2019direct].

**Separability.** With λ = 0 the optimum exists iff the data overlap [albert1984existence]; random labels are separable with probability 1/2 at n = 2d [cover1965geometrical], and with signal the threshold moves to d/n = h_MLE(signal) < 1/2 [candes2020phase]; on separable data gradient descent diverges in norm like log t toward the max-margin direction while the loss decays as 1/t [soudry2018implicit]. DegNSEP/DegSEP [freund2018condition] certify (non-)separability by an LP and bound the curvature at the optimum. Practical rules: keep d/n ≪ 1 (or prescribe spectra), check separability before small-λ sweeps, and remember that on nearly separable data ‖η*‖ grows like log(1/λ) so that μ_eff collapses toward λ while L_eff falls below R^2/4.

**Hard instances.** For (A1)/(A4) the implementable instances are the block-separable chain quadratics of [lan2018optimal] (tridiagonal A with last entry κ_A = (√Q + 3)/(√Q + 1), per-block condition number Q = κ/n in Bach's average convention, x*_{i,j} = q^j, dimension at least n(m, k) to observe the bound over k iterations), the explicit variant of [zhang2020tight] (ξ = (√((κ − 1)/n + 1) + 3)/(√((κ − 1)/n + 1) + 1)), the Hessian-partitioned instance of [han2024lower] (rows of B(m, 0, ζ) assigned mod n; the activated support T_k is a direct observable), and the 2 × 2 coupled quadratics of [arjevani2016dimension] as a cheap sanity check; the Nesterov chain itself [nesterov2004introductory] is the atom. The resisting-oracle instance of [agarwal2015lower] is algorithm-dependent and the dead-zone instance of [woodworth2016tight] needs dimension ~ κn/ε, so neither is suitable for numerics. [hendrikx2021optimal] (single node) gives a ready-made inequality E‖θ^t − θ*‖^2 ≥ c(1 − 2m/(m + √(mκ_s/3)))^{4t/m} to overlay on trajectories, and [pedregosa2020acceleration] a recipe for random quadratics with prescribed spectral density.

**High-precision optimum.** Use Newton with backtracking and the Newton decrement λ(x)^2 ≤ 0.68 as a certificate f(x) − p* ≤ λ(x)^2 [boyd2004convex] (quadratic phase from the region ν ≤ λ^{1/2}/(2R) of [bach2010self]; damped-Newton steps without line search for generalized self-concordant losses in [sun2019generalized]); TRON [lin2008trust] / LIBLINEAR [fan2008liblinear] (C = 1/(nλ), tolerance lowered to ~10^{-10}) for large sparse data; proximal Newton for composite variants [lee2014proximal]; CG/L-BFGS beyond a few thousand dimensions [minka2003comparison]. [kovalev2020dont] computes x* by gradient descent, which is a weak point to improve. Dataset conventions (a9a, w8a, ijcnn1, covtype, rcv1, mushrooms, phishing) are in [chang2011libsvm].

## 8. Gap analysis and positioning

**A1 (lower bound and its refinement).** *Established:* Ω((n + √(nκ)) log(1/ε)) for span-based methods with a fixed sampling distribution [lan2018optimal], for oblivious fixed-step methods such as SAGA with γ = 1/(4R^2) [arjevani2016dimension], with proximal oracles and small dimension [xie2019general, han2024lower]; the n log(1/ε) term is specific to i.i.d. sampling plus span [hannah2018breaking]; the deterministic case has only an additive n [agarwal2015lower]; the no-span randomized case keeps √(nκ) log(1/ε) plus an additive n [woodworth2016tight]. *Open or only partial:* the "arbitrary index sequence" statement is proved only for full-gradient/loopless protocols [hannah2018breaking, han2024lower] and, under a zero-chain property, in an unrefereed preprint [zhang2020tight]; nothing beyond Ω(n) is known for fully adaptive sequences in the small-κ regime (PL only, [peng2026dense]); the n^{3/4} bound under average smoothness has an open regime L/μ = O(√n) [zhang2020tight]. *Risk:* quoting the second sentence as a theorem about "arbitrary index sequences" would be wrong; the risk of mis-citation (ar5iv renumbering of [agarwal2015lower] and [lan2018optimal]; deterministic-only erratum) is high. *Suggestions:* state A1 in Bach's notation starting from Assumption 2.1.4 / Theorem 2.1.13 of [nesterov2018lectures], follow the proof of Theorem 3 of [lan2018optimal] (multinomial count of per-block evaluations, convexity of q^{2k_i}), use the zero-chain vocabulary of [carmon2020lower], and present the refinement as Theorems 2/4 of [hannah2018breaking] plus Theorem E.6 of [han2024lower].

**A2 (naive acceleration).** *Established:* the naive method is [nitanda2014stochastic]; its complexity O((n + min{κ, n√κ}) log(1/ε)) [nitanda2016accelerated] shows no acceleration for b = 1; the mechanisms are error accumulation with weight k [schmidt2011convergence, devolder2014first], the unabsorbable inner product in the tight variance bound [allenzhu2018katyusha, zhou2019direct], and the need for μ-dependent non-stationary coefficients [arjevani2016iteration, arjevani2017limitations]; empirical failure with τ_2 = 0 [allenzhu2018katyusha] and with plain stochastic gradients [kidambi2018insufficiency]. *Open:* there is no lower bound in the list showing that Nesterov + SVRG with b = 1 *cannot* be accelerated; the statement is "not known to accelerate" and the failing term is identified only inside specific proofs. *Risk:* claiming impossibility. *Suggestions:* reproduce the one-step analysis of linear coupling [allenzhu2017linear] with the SVRG estimator and exhibit the term ⟨∇f(x_{k+1}), x̃ − x_{k+1}⟩ that is left over; reproduce Figure 1 of [allenzhu2018katyusha] (τ_2 = 0) and Acc-Prox-SVRG with b = 1 versus b large on the hard instance; use the SDP of [hu2018dissipativity] to show infeasibility of a certificate without the reference-point coupling.

**A3 (corrected acceleration).** *Established:* Katyusha, MiG, SSNM, L-Katyusha and their Lyapunov proofs [allenzhu2018katyusha, zhou2018simple, zhou2019direct, kovalev2020dont, qian2021lsvrg]; the "magnet" interpretation; the SDP certificate [hu2018dissipativity]; alternatives without negative momentum (Point-SAGA, RPDG, APCG, SPDC, Catalyst) and the log-factor price of indirect acceleration [lin2018catalyst]; the n^{2/3} price of dropping the reference point [driggs2022accelerating]. *Open:* no reference rewrites L-Katyusha with Bach's gradient-memory Lyapunov (the L-Katyusha potential uses function values at y and w); SSNM is the only accelerated method whose Lyapunov is SAGA's table-based one, and it uses the Bregman rather than the gradient-norm table term. *Risk:* low in content (reproduction), moderate in novelty — the value is in the mapping of each lemma of [kovalev2020dont] to a step of Proposition 5.9 and in the explicit absorption bookkeeping. *Suggestions:* follow Lemmas 1–4 of [kovalev2020dont] for L-SVRG first (identical structure to V_t), then Lemmas 6–10 (4.1–4.5 in the arXiv version) for L-Katyusha; cross-check constants against [qian2021lsvrg] (expected-smoothness form), [kulunchakov2019estimate] (estimate sequence) and [li2021anita]; cite [arjevani2016dimension, woodworth2016tight] for optimality.

**A4 (empirical exponents).** *Established:* qualitative evidence for √κ versus κ [zhou2019direct] on two λ values; gains only for ill-conditioned problems [lin2018catalyst, allenzhu2018katyusha, kulunchakov2019estimate]; SVRG matches accelerated methods when κ ≪ n [song2020variance, hannah2018breaking]; the hard instance with closed-form optimum and per-block decay q^{2k_i} [lan2018optimal, zhang2020tight]. *Open:* no controlled exponent fit on a lower-bound instance exists in the list; SAGA versus L-Katyusha on such an instance has not been reported [kovalev2020dont]. *Risk:* the hard instance needs dimension growing with the number of iterations (n(m, k) in [lan2018optimal]; [nemirovsky1983problem]), the Gaussian-design alternative has κ set by d/n [marchenko1967distribution], and on logistic regression local curvature contaminates the exponent (Section 6.3). *Suggestions:* implement the block instance of [lan2018optimal] / [zhang2020tight] with per-block Q = κ/n and the Hessian-partitioned one of [han2024lower] (log the activated support T_k); sweep κ over three decades at fixed n with κ/n from 10^{-1} to 10^{3} to separate the two regimes; fit gradients-to-ε against κ on log–log axes; overlay the single-node bound of [hendrikx2021optimal]; add MiG/SSNM as second accelerated baselines; report test error only for the statistically meaningful λ range [sridharan2008fast, marteauferey2019beyond].

**B1 (preconditioned SAGA).** *Established:* the Euclidean proof transfers to a fixed quadratic metric by change of variables, with R^2 → L_P = max_i λ_max(P^{-1/2}∇^2f_i P^{-1/2}) and μ → μ_P [lu2018relatively, bauschke2017descent, kolte2015accelerating, gonen2016solving]; Bregman-SAGA Corollary 1 gives 1 − min{1/(2n), 1/(8κ_{f/h})} with step 1/(8L_{f/h}) [dragomir2021fast]; preconditioned SVRG/Katyusha X and PROMISE give κ_f^M and quadratic-regularity constants, with the per-component cocoercivity (through the Hessian dissimilarity τ for b = 1) as the surviving loose term [liu2019acceleration, frangella2024promise, frangella2024sketchysgd, gower2019rsn]; eigenvalue-bound-only analyses are provably loose [moritz2016linearly, sadiev2024stochastic, lucchi2015variance]; worst-case rates cannot improve [arjevani2017oracle, han2024lower]; no acceleration under non-quadratic relative smoothness [dragomir2022optimal]. *Open:* no paper in the list writes the gradient-memory Lyapunov (Bach's V_t with Δ = 3γ^2) in the P-norm, and none measures which of the square-norm, cocoercivity, variance, strong-convexity steps changes most under a given P on real geometries. *Risk:* low if the project restricts to fixed quadratic P; the statement "which steps become loose" is only interesting if P is not folded into the data, i.e. if the proof keeps Euclidean cocoercivity (as the quasi-Newton papers do) — this should be made explicit. *Suggestions:* write V_t^P = ‖θ_t − η*‖_P^2 + Δ Σ_i‖z_i − f_i'(η*)‖_{P^{-1}}^2, rewrite (5.31) in the P^{-1} norm (expected smoothness in the P^{-1} norm as in Proposition 16 of [frangella2024promise] and Assumption 3.1 of [gower2021stochastic] with W = P), and compare three analyses on the same P: change-of-variables (exact), eigenvalue-bound (cond(P) factor), and expected-smoothness (data-dependent); use Nyström [frangella2023randomized] or diagonal Hutchinson [sadiev2024stochastic] P with O(rd)/O(d) cost; connect L_P for rank-one f_i to ridge leverage scores and coherence [gonen2016solving, agarwal2020leverage, wang2018giant].

**B2 (trajectory-dependent constants).** *Established:* μ_eff is the restricted secant constant [zhang2013gradient]; linear rates hold under one-point conditions [karimi2016linear, necoara2019linear, gower2019sgd, hu2017unified]; local curvature of logistic regression is governed by H(η*) and self-concordance [bach2010self, bach2014adaptivity, sun2019generalized, freund2018condition]; local regimes of SAGA [poon2018local]; published anecdotes of "hidden strong convexity" [lin2015universal, kulunchakov2019estimate, zhou2019direct]. *Open:* no measurement of μ_eff(t) along SAGA trajectories; acceleration with μ_eff instead of μ is not covered by any theorem (one-point conditions forbid acceleration in general [guilleescuret2022gradient]; oblivious methods need μ [arjevani2017limitations]). *Risk:* μ_eff(t) is a single scalar that mixes the direction of θ_t − η* with the spectrum; it can be large while the Lyapunov contraction is governed by the memory term; average-case behaviour depends on the whole spectral density [pedregosa2020acceleration]. *Suggestions:* report μ_eff(t) together with λ_min(H(η*)), the Rayleigh quotient of H(θ_t) in the direction θ_t − η*, the quadratic-regularity ratio of [frangella2024promise], DegNSEP [freund2018condition] and the Dikin radius 1/R of [bach2010self]; use the generalized self-concordance bound e^{±R‖θ_t − θ_s‖} [sun2019generalized] to predict how fast μ_eff can move per step; test whether replacing μ by μ_eff in the SDP of [hu2017unified] (sector condition at x*) predicts the observed contraction.

**B3 (instrumented proof).** *Established:* the exact list of inequalities to instrument [bach2024learning]; their generic form (A, B, C, ρ) [gorbunov2020unified] and the explicit trade-off ρ*(K), K = 4κq/n, predicting the bottleneck per (n, κ) regime [hofmann2015variance]; the theoretical counterpart of a tightness ratio (dual multipliers) [goujaud2023fundamental, taylor2018lyapunov]; software to compute the exact one-step worst case for a given Lyapunov structure [goujaud2024pepit]; the data-dependent constants that replace R^2 and L_max [gower2021stochastic, gower2019sgd, needell2016stochastic, gazagnadou2019optimal]; the protocol for controlling anisotropy and spectra [dieuleveut2017harder, liao2021hessian, hsu2014random] and for the reference optimum [boyd2004convex]; a model of closing a gap by changing the Lyapunov function [sebbouh2019towards]. *Open:* no paper computes per-inequality slack along trajectories; the worst case of Bach's gradient-memory Lyapunov with Δ = 3γ^2 and γ = 1/(4R^2) has not been computed (PEPit's SAGA example uses the Bregman potential). *Risk:* a ratio measured at one iterate is noisy (sampling) and conflates worst-case over functions with one trajectory; the "min of two rates" step is not an inequality on data but on constants. *Suggestions:* define, per step t, the ratio of the right-hand side to the left-hand side of each of (i) the square-norm step, (ii) cocoercivity (5.31) with R^2 versus the empirical E_i‖f_i'(θ) − f_i'(η*)‖^2/((θ − η*)^TF'(θ)), (iii) variance ≤ second moment, (iv) strong convexity (μ versus μ_eff(t)), (v) the memory recursion (1 − 1/n) step, and (vi) the Lipschitz initialisation; average over the sampled index; encode V_t in PEPit (and in the LMI of [hu2017unified]) to obtain the worst-case contraction and the multipliers; compare the measured one-step contraction of V_t with 1 − min{1/(3n), 3μ/(16R^2)} and with the PEP value; vary (n, κ, anisotropy) on the generators of Section 7 and on the Bach book experiment (d = 40, λ = R^2/n, n = 10^3 and 10^4) first.

## 9. Recommended reading order and open questions

1. [bach2024learning], Section 5.4.4 and Proposition 5.9 — the object of study; then [gower2020variance] for the landscape and the catalogue of what is unproven.
2. [defazio2014saga] and [hofmann2015variance] — the two SAGA proofs (Bregman versus gradient-memory Lyapunov) and the first explicit "min of two rates" trade-off.
3. [gorbunov2020unified] — the (A, B, C, ρ) template that turns the proof into four constants.
4. [kovalev2020dont] — L-SVRG and L-Katyusha, the lemmas to reproduce in Bach's style; [allenzhu2017linear] and [allenzhu2018katyusha] (Sections 1, 5, 7.1) for the mechanism.
5. [lan2018optimal] (Theorem 3 and the instance) and [hannah2018breaking] — the lower bound and its refinement; [han2024lower] for the refereed, loopless-protocol version.
6. [nitanda2014stochastic] with [nitanda2016accelerated] and [schmidt2011convergence] — why naive acceleration fails.
7. [zhou2019direct] — SAGA accelerated with its own Lyapunov function and the only published κ-exponent remark.
8. [dragomir2021fast] with [lu2018relatively] and [frangella2024promise] — preconditioned SAGA and the constants that replace R^2 and μ.
9. [hu2017unified] and [goujaud2024pepit] with [goujaud2023fundamental] — computer-aided tightness of SAGA-type Lyapunov analyses.
10. [bach2010self], [zhang2013gradient] and [karimi2016linear] — local curvature of logistic regression and the dictionary of effective-curvature conditions; then [liao2021hessian] and [dieuleveut2017harder] for the geometry generators.

**Open questions raised by the list.** (1) What is the exact worst-case one-step contraction of Bach's gradient-memory Lyapunov V_t (Δ = 3γ^2) and which of the six inequalities carries the dual multiplier?
